# San Antonio Manufactured Home Transport: Distance-Based Pricing Tiers for 2026

**Moving a manufactured home from San Antonio costs between $3,000 and $55,000+, depending on distance, home type, and service scope.** Texas's position as the nation's largest manufactured housing market — with **18,343 new homes shipped in 2024** and over 1.3 million units statewide — creates fierce transporter competition that keeps San Antonio-area pricing **10–20% below national averages**. A flat $40 TxDMV permit fee per section (with no distance escalation), diesel prices roughly **$0.55/gallon below the national average**, and high-volume Texas Triangle corridors all contribute to this cost advantage. However, navigating San Antonio's restrictive oversize-load regulations — particularly the blanket prohibition on oversize transit through Loop 410 — adds route-planning complexity that can affect timing and cost.

## Per-mile rate components and how they build

The building blocks of manufactured home transport pricing break down into distinct per-mile components that stack predictably. The **towing vehicle (toter truck)** charges **$4.00–$5.50 per mile**, representing the single largest variable cost. Escort/pilot car vehicles add **$1.50–$3.50 per mile per vehicle**, with high-pole escort cars (for height clearance scouting) running **$2.25–$2.50 per mile**. These rates apply per section — a double-wide requires two toter trucks and, if escorts are needed, the convoy can share escort vehicles under Texas's 1,000-foot convoy provision.

The transport-only per-mile rate for a single-wide with one escort works out to roughly **$5.50–$7.50 per mile**. For a double-wide convoyed (two sections sharing escorts), the effective rate is **$9.50–$13.00 per mile**. Industry sources commonly cite an all-in blended rate (transport + escorts + permits amortized) of **$6–$15 per mile per section** for long-distance moves, consistent with data from This Old House (2026), TX Mobile Home Buyer, and Pricing Van Lines (2025).

Additional cost components that factor into all-in pricing include prep/teardown at **$1,500–$2,500** (disconnecting utilities, removing skirting, separating sections), setup at destination at **$1,000–$5,000 per section** (leveling, blocking, anchoring, reconnection), hitch welding at **$200–$300** for single-wide or **$400–$600** for double-wide halves, and tire replacement at roughly **$50 per tire** or axle repair at **$100–$200 per axle** when needed.

## TxDMV permits: flat fees that don't scale with distance

One of the most significant cost advantages for Texas-based moves is the state's remarkably simple and inexpensive permit structure. The **TxDMV manufactured housing single-trip permit costs a flat $40 per section** — the same fee whether the move covers 5 miles or 500 miles. There is **no per-mile surcharge, no per-county fee, and no highway maintenance fee** (unless the load exceeds 80,000 lbs gross vehicle weight, which is unusual for standard manufactured homes). This was confirmed in the TxDMV May 2025 Motor Carrier Handbook and the TxDMV Table of Permit Fees.

Each section of a multi-section home requires its own permit, so total permit costs are simply:

- **Single-wide:** $40 (one section)
- **Double-wide:** $80 (two sections × $40)
- **Triple-wide:** $120 (three sections × $40)

These fees are dramatically lower than many other states' permit structures and contribute to Texas's overall transport cost advantage. For comparison, a General Single-Trip Permit (required if the load is overweight) costs $60 at legal weight, escalating to $210–$470 for heavier loads. The Annual Manufactured Housing Permit costs **$1,500/year** but is limited to factory-to-storage moves within 20 miles of the manufacturing facility — relevant mainly for factory operations near Seguin.

Permits are issued through the TxPROS online system, valid for one movement within five days. **No transport is permitted on six national holidays** (New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas).

## Escort and pilot car costs by width threshold

Texas applies **manufactured-housing-specific escort thresholds** that differ from general oversize load rules, creating meaningful cost differences depending on the actual width of each section including eaves overhang.

| MH section width | Escort requirement | Estimated cost per 100 miles |
|---|---|---|
| **≤16 feet** | No escort required | $0 |
| **>16 ft to ≤18 ft** | 1 escort (front on 2-lane, rear on 4+ lane) | $150–$350 |
| **>18 feet** | 2 escorts (front and rear, all highways) | $300–$700 |

Most standard single-wide homes (14–16 ft body width) fall at or below the 16-foot threshold, meaning **no escort is required** — a significant cost savings. However, eave overhangs can push the effective transport width beyond 16 feet, triggering the one-escort requirement. Double-wide sections transported individually are typically 14–16 feet each, also often avoiding escort requirements.

Texas law allows **two sections of a multi-section home to convoy and share escorts** when the distance between units does not exceed 1,000 feet. This convoy provision can cut escort costs nearly in half for double-wide moves where both halves exceed 16 feet.

**Texas does not generally require DPS (law enforcement) escorts** for manufactured home transport. The only identified exception in the San Antonio TxDMV district is certain routes in Bandera County (SH 16 between SH 173 and Enchanted River Dr for loads over 17 feet high), which require a Bandera County Sheriff escort. Private pilot car operators handle virtually all escort duties.

Market rates for private pilot/escort cars (Heavy Haulers, HomeGuide, Angi data from 2025–2026): **$1.50–$2.00/mile** for standard lead or chase; **$2.25–$2.50/mile** for high-pole vehicles; **$400–$500/day** minimum for short runs.

## San Antonio's oversize-load route restrictions are uniquely challenging

The San Antonio TxDMV district maintains some of the most restrictive oversize-load regulations of any Texas metro, creating route-planning challenges that directly affect transport timing and cost. These restrictions are documented in the TxDMV San Antonio District Permit Restrictions file (updated on a rolling basis, with entries as recent as January 2026).

**The critical rule: no oversize transit through Loop 410.** Restriction #19802 (permanent, dated November 29, 2023) states: *"No width, no height, and/or 65' max length on all highways inside IH 410 unless the vehicle has an origin or destination here."* Any manufactured home exceeding the legal width of 8'6" — which is virtually all of them — cannot transit through the Loop 410 perimeter. Homes must have an origin or destination within the loop, or they must route around it entirely. A limited detour exception exists for loads up to 12 feet wide, 15 feet high, and 80 feet long via IH 10W → E.US 87S → E.IH 410.

**I-35 through San Antonio is severely restricted.** I-35 northbound between IH 10 and Rittiman Rd has a permanent **12-foot maximum width** restriction. The IH 10/IH 35/US 87 northbound to IH 10/US 87 westbound interchange carries a blanket **"NO PERMITS"** designation (as of August 2025). The massive **I-35 NEX construction program** — a $1.5 billion+ expansion project running from N. Walters Street to FM 1103 (~20 miles through NE San Antonio) — imposes frequent temporary closures through at least fall 2027. The I-35 NEX Central section (I-410 North to FM 3009 in Schertz) is over halfway complete; the South Phase 1 started in summer 2024 and targets late 2028 completion.

**Loop 1604 interchanges carry tight dimensional limits.** The junction of IH 10/US 87 and N. LP 1604 imposes permanent **12-foot max width and/or 80-foot max length** restrictions across multiple ramp movements. Some connections carry "NO PERMITS" designations (updated April 2025). Loop 1604 frontage roads near IH 10 have a **10-foot max width** restriction, and the N. LP 1604 westbound collector/distributor road is limited to **11-foot max width** near Leon Creek.

**IH-10 has time-restricted segments.** Between Camp Bullis Rd and De Zavala Rd, oversize loads face an **11-foot max width during 5:00 AM–9:00 PM Monday through Friday**, with no permits at all during other times and on weekends (dated May 2025). The IH 10 westbound exit ramp to La Cantera Pkwy has a permanent 12-foot width and 16-foot height limit.

**US-281 access is limited.** No permits are issued between US 181 and IH 410 (permanent restriction). The IH 37/US 281 northbound exit ramp to Marbach Rd also carries a permanent no-permits designation.

**Time-of-day restrictions** apply universally: oversize movement is limited to **30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes after sunset**, including weekends. Night travel is only permitted for loads ≤10 feet wide, ≤14 feet high, and ≤100 feet long on the Interstate system. Movement is prohibited during hazardous weather conditions. Transporters should contact the San Antonio TxDMV District at SAT.OSOW.Permits@TxDOT.gov or (210) 615-5912 for current construction zone updates.

## Interstate permit stacking adds modest but real costs

When manufactured homes cross state lines from San Antonio, each state requires its own separate permit. **No interstate reciprocity agreements exist** for oversize/overweight permits between Texas and its neighbors. The permit fees themselves are relatively modest, but escort requirement differences between states can significantly alter total costs.

### Texas + Louisiana (SA → Shreveport, Baton Rouge, New Orleans)

| Fee component | Single-wide | Double-wide (2 sections) |
|---|---|---|
| TxDMV MH permit ($40/section) | $40 | $80 |
| LaDOTD mobile home oversize permit ($10/trip, 72-hr validity) | $10 | $20 |
| LA escort vehicle permit (out-of-state company) | $10 | $10 |
| **Total state permit fees** | **~$60** | **~$110** |

Louisiana's permit structure is notably inexpensive at **$10 per trip** for mobile home oversize permits (LaDOTD, Act 301 of 2019). However, Louisiana requires **State Police escorts for any load exceeding 16 feet wide** on any highway — more restrictive than Texas's private-escort-only system. Louisiana also restricts loads wider than 12 feet from Interstates through Shreveport, Monroe, Lake Charles, Baton Rouge, and New Orleans during rush hours (7–9 AM, 3:30–5 PM M–F). Private escort operators in Louisiana must carry **$500,000 in general liability, motor vehicle liability, and workers' comp coverage** (La. R.S. 32:387.1). Mobile homes over 14 feet wide can travel Louisiana Interstates — an exception to the general rule prohibiting >14-foot loads on Interstate highways.

### Texas + New Mexico (SA → Albuquerque, Las Cruces)

| Fee component | Single-wide | Double-wide (2 sections) |
|---|---|---|
| TxDMV MH permit ($40/section) | $40 | $80 |
| NMDOT single-trip oversize permit | $25 | $50 |
| NM transaction fee + 5% service charge | ~$14 | ~$15 |
| **Total state permit fees** | **~$79** | **~$145** |

New Mexico charges a **$25 single-trip oversize permit** plus a **$12 transaction fee and 5% service charge** on all permits. Self-issue permit books are available at **$250 for 10 permits** ($25 each). A critical additional cost: NM State Police escorts, when required, cost **$300/day**. Loads up to 18 feet wide on designated 4-lane highways may not require escorts, but 2-lane highways require front and rear escorts for loads over 18 feet. If the move originates within New Mexico, a **property tax clearance certificate** is required from the county assessor. All commercial vehicles must stop at operating NM ports of entry.

### Texas + Oklahoma (SA → Oklahoma City, Tulsa)

| Fee component | Single-wide | Double-wide (2 sections) |
|---|---|---|
| TxDMV MH permit ($40/section) | $40 | $80 |
| ODOT manufactured home permit | $40 | $80 |
| OK processing/credit card fees | ~$4 | ~$5 |
| **Total state permit fees** | **~$84** | **~$165** |

Oklahoma's **$40 manufactured home single-trip permit** mirrors Texas's pricing. Oklahoma's escort thresholds are more restrictive: on 2-lane highways, **one front escort is required at widths exceeding 12 feet**, and two escorts (front and rear) are required above 14 feet. On multi-lane highways, one rear escort is needed above 14 feet. Oklahoma restricts oversize loads during rush hours in Cleveland, Oklahoma, and Tulsa counties (7–9 AM, 3:30–6:30 PM M–F). The ODOT annual envelope permit ($4,000) is explicitly **void for manufactured home use**.

### Multi-state permit cost summary

Total government permit fees for interstate moves from San Antonio are remarkably low — typically **$60–$170 for a double-wide** depending on destination state. The real cost escalation comes from **differing escort requirements** across state lines. A double-wide that requires no escorts in Texas may need State Police escorts in Louisiana or multiple private escorts in Oklahoma, adding hundreds to thousands of dollars to the move.

## Fuel surcharges and San Antonio's diesel price advantage

San Antonio's proximity to Gulf Coast refineries delivers a consistent fuel cost advantage for manufactured home transporters. As of March 2026, **San Antonio diesel prices average approximately $4.52/gallon** (AAA/Stacker data, March 16, 2026), compared to the **PADD 3 (Gulf Coast) average of $4.84** and the **national average of $5.07** (EIA Gasoline and Diesel Fuel Update, released March 17, 2026). San Antonio runs roughly **$0.55/gallon below the national average** — a structural advantage driven by refinery proximity, lower Texas fuel taxes, and conventional (non-reformulated) fuel blends.

A manufactured home toter truck towing a loaded home typically achieves **4–6 MPG** (estimated based on Class 7–8 truck fuel consumption data from Geotab, OOIDA, and industry sources; the extreme wind drag from a 14–16 foot wide, 50–72 foot long structure significantly reduces fuel economy versus standard freight). At 5 MPG, San Antonio's $0.55/gallon diesel advantage translates to roughly **$0.11 per mile in fuel savings** versus the national average. On a 500-mile move, that's approximately $55 in savings per truck — meaningful but modest relative to total move costs.

| Diesel price (March 2026) | Fuel cost/mile at 5 MPG | vs. national average |
|---|---|---|
| **San Antonio: $4.52** | $0.90/mile | Saves $0.11/mile |
| Texas average: $4.61 | $0.92/mile | Saves $0.09/mile |
| PADD 3 Gulf Coast: $4.84 | $0.97/mile | Saves $0.05/mile |
| National average: $5.07 | $1.01/mile | Baseline |
| West Coast: $5.86 | $1.17/mile | Costs $0.16 more |
| California: $6.43 | $1.29/mile | Costs $0.27 more |

Most Texas manufactured home transporters **incorporate fuel costs into their all-in per-mile quotes** rather than breaking out a separate fuel surcharge line item. There is no industry-standard separate fuel surcharge specific to manufactured home transport. When surcharges are calculated, the standard trucking formula applies: **(Current EIA Diesel − Baseline Price) ÷ Truck MPG = surcharge per mile**. Using the DAT standard $1.25 baseline and San Antonio diesel of $4.52: ($4.52 − $1.25) ÷ 5 MPG = **$0.65/mile implied surcharge**, versus $0.76/mile at national average diesel.

Fuel represents an estimated **15–25% of transport-only cost** and **5–15% of full-service (all-in) cost**, since labor, setup, and equipment dominate total pricing. The March 2026 diesel prices reflect a sharp spike from early 2026 levels (PADD 3 was ~$3.16 in January 2026, per FRED/EIA) — an increase of approximately 53% in two months that transporters are actively passing through to customers.

## Texas Triangle corridors create 15–25% pricing discounts

The three major transport corridors connecting San Antonio to Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, and Austin — the "Texas Triangle" — benefit from concentrated transporter competition and high move volume that pushes per-mile rates **an estimated 15–25% below comparable rural or low-traffic routes**.

**SA → Austin (I-35, ~80 miles)** is the most competitive corridor. The Cavco Industries factory in **Seguin** (35 miles east of SA on I-10) ships the lion's share of its production into the SA–Austin corridor, creating predictable, recurring transport demand. Multiple dealer clusters in New Braunfels (Titan Factory Direct), Seguin (Texas Built Mobile Homes, Factory Expo), and San Antonio (Alamo Homes, Clayton Homes, Manufactured Housing Consultants) generate constant short-haul traffic. Single-wide transport-only costs on this corridor run **$2,500–$5,000**, with full-service at **$5,000–$8,000** — at the low end of the 50–100 mile tier due to competition.

**SA → Houston (I-10, ~200 miles)** connects Texas's 2nd and 4th largest metros with heavy manufactured home activity in both regions. Multiple companies serve this route: Martinez Mobile Homes, Texas Elite MHS, Texas Mobile Home Transport, and national brokers. Houston-area average new double-wide prices were $115,400 in Q1 2025. Single-wide full-service costs run **$7,000–$14,000** for this corridor.

**SA → DFW (I-35, ~275 miles)** benefits from factory-to-dealer flows: Champion Home Builders operates plants in Burleson and Mansfield (both near DFW), while Seguin-area factories ship northbound. The corridor passes through Austin and Waco, where intermediate pickups can reduce deadhead costs. Single-wide full-service costs run **$8,000–$15,000**.

**Rural and remote route premiums** offset the Triangle advantage. Moves to West Texas (Permian Basin), the Rio Grande Valley, or the Panhandle carry **20–40% higher per-mile costs** due to longer deadhead distances, fewer competing transporters, and more complex routing. The fundamental problem in manufactured home transport is that **every return trip is deadhead** — the specialized toter cannot carry general cargo back. On Triangle corridors, transporters mitigate this by scheduling back-to-back jobs, running multi-stop circuits, and maintaining volume contracts with dealers.

The current market conditions amplify competitive pricing. Per Mobile ByeBye's 2025 market analysis and TMHA survey data: **14,700 unsold units sit on Texas dealer lots** (nearly double the 7,900 in mid-2022), factory lead times have dropped to 6 weeks, and **71% of retailers have cut prices at least twice since January 2024**. Many dealers now offer **factory-paid delivery within 150–250 miles** as a competitive incentive, effectively absorbing $4,000–$8,000+ in transport costs into home pricing. Transportation crews report available capacity, giving buyers leverage in negotiating transport rates.

## Conclusion: what these numbers mean for pricing decisions

Three structural factors make San Antonio one of the most cost-effective origins (or destinations) for manufactured home transport in the country. First, the **$40 flat permit fee with zero distance escalation** means government costs are negligible at any distance — a stark contrast to states with mileage-based or county-based permit structures. Second, **escort requirements are permissive**: most standard single-wide and properly-sectioned double-wide homes (≤16 ft per section) require no escorts at all in Texas, eliminating what would otherwise be a $1.50–$3.50/mile addition. Third, the **Texas Triangle corridor competition and current buyer's-market glut** create favorable negotiating conditions for consumers.

The most important route-planning takeaway is the **Loop 410 transit prohibition** — any move crossing San Antonio without an origin or destination inside the loop must route around the entire metro perimeter, potentially adding 20–40 miles and requiring careful coordination with TxDMV's San Antonio district. I-35 NEX construction through 2027–2028 further constrains northbound oversize routing.

For interstate moves, the permit stacking costs are modest ($60–$170 total for a double-wide), but differing state escort requirements — particularly Louisiana's State Police escort mandate for loads over 16 feet — can materially increase total costs. Fuel savings from San Antonio's low diesel prices ($0.11/mile versus national average) provide a small but consistent per-mile advantage that compounds over long distances.

These figures should be treated as market ranges rather than firm quotes; actual pricing varies with home condition, age, season, specific route, and transporter availability. All data is current as of early 2026, sourced from TxDMV fee schedules (May 2025 Motor Carrier Handbook), EIA diesel data (March 17, 2026), TMHA market reports (February 2026), and multiple transport company and cost-platform sources dated 2024–2026.
