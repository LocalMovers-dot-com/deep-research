# Manufactured home transport permitting in San Antonio: eight regulatory layers decoded

**Moving a manufactured home within, into, or out of the San Antonio metro area requires navigating at least eight overlapping regulatory frameworks — state housing authority licensing, oversize-load highway permits, escort requirements, local building permits, floodplain compliance, and potentially interstate or international clearances.** The total regulatory burden is substantial: a single intrastate move of a standard singlewide home triggers a minimum of a $40 TxDMV highway permit, a $75 TDHCA installation-reporting fee, and a $78 City of San Antonio installation permit — plus the installer's $25,000 surety bond and $300,000 liability insurance must already be in place. A doublewide crossing from Louisiana adds separate per-section permits in each state. This guide details every layer with statutory citations, current fee amounts, processing timelines, and filing portals.

---

## 1. TDHCA manufactured housing division: the state licensing foundation

The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs governs manufactured home installation through **Texas Administrative Code Title 10, Part 1, Chapter 80**, with statutory authority under **Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1201**. The rules are organized across seven subchapters: Subchapter A (§§80.1–80.3) covers fees and administration; Subchapter B (§§80.20–80.26) sets installation standards; Subchapter C (§§80.30–80.38) defines licensee responsibilities; Subchapter D (§§80.40–80.41) governs licensing; and Subchapters E through G cover enforcement, consumer claims, and statements of ownership. The most recent rule amendments took effect **March 3, 2024** (49 TexReg 521), with further licensing amendments effective **July 14, 2024** (49 TexReg 4429).

**Installer license requirements** demand completion of a 12-hour education program (8-hour Core Licensing Education Course plus 4-hour Installer LEC), passage of both the Core exam and Installer exam with scores of 70% or above, a fingerprint-based criminal background check, and filing of a **$25,000 surety bond** with TDHCA. No prior experience is required. The biennial license fee is **$350**, and the education courses cost $150 (Core) plus $50 (Installer), with exam fees of $85 (Core) and $70 (Installer) paid to PSI Exams. Continuing education of 8 hours per renewal cycle is mandated under Occupations Code §1201.113. New license applications are processed in approximately **7 working days** once all materials are submitted (per TDHCA Form 1017, rev. 11/21/2023).

The installation reporting fee under TAC §80.3(b) is **$75 for the first section** and **$25 for each additional section**. The Notice of Installation (Form T) must be submitted to TDHCA within 7 days of installation completion. Statement of Ownership transactions cost **$55 each** (Form 1022, rev. 09/01/2017), processed within 15 working days per Occupations Code §1201.207(a). Salvaged home plan review and inspection costs **$200** plus mileage. Homeowners can verify any installer's license status through TDHCA's online License View portal at https://mhweb.tdhca.state.tx.us/mhweb/license_view.jsp, which is updated regularly (last confirmed update: 02/27/2026). The main manufactured housing division hub is at https://www.tdhca.texas.gov/mhd.

## 2. TxDMV oversize permits and the TxPROS routing system

Highway transport permits for manufactured homes fall under **Texas Transportation Code Chapter 623, Subchapter E (§§623.091–623.105)** — a dedicated subchapter separate from general oversize/overweight provisions. A critical jurisdictional note: **permitting authority transferred from TxDOT to the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (TxDMV) on January 1, 2012**, though TxDOT retains control over route restrictions and engineering studies.

The **single-trip manufactured housing permit** costs **$40** (§623.096(a)), is valid for one movement not exceeding 5 days, and may be issued to TDHCA-licensed manufacturers, retailers, or installers, registered motor carriers, or homeowners whose name matches the title. The **annual manufactured housing permit** costs **$1,500** (§623.096(b)) but is limited to transporting new homes from manufacturing facilities to temporary storage within 20 miles. Both permit types are filed through **TxPROS (Texas Permitting & Routing Optimization System)** at https://txpros.txdmv.gov/ or by phone at (800) 299-1700. Standard self-issued permits process **within minutes**; complex route studies may take days to weeks.

Dimensional limits and escort thresholds are critical for San Antonio-area moves. Legal travel without a permit requires staying within **8'6" wide, 14' high, and 65' long** (combination). Under manufactured housing permits, the maximum dimensions are **20' wide, 16' high, and 110' overall length**. Escort requirements follow §623.099 exclusively — not the general oversize rules in 43 TAC §219.11(k), which explicitly exclude manufactured housing:

- **Up to 16' wide**: No escort required
- **Over 16' to 18' wide**: One escort (front on two-lane roads, rear on multi-lane highways)
- **Over 18' wide**: Two escorts, front and rear

Loads exceeding **16' wide** cannot use main lanes of controlled-access highways and must use frontage roads. The speed limit during transport is **55 mph on highways** and **45 mph on county roads/city streets** (§623.098). Transport is restricted to **daylight hours** (half-hour before sunrise to half-hour after sunset) and is **prohibited on six national holidays**: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, and Christmas.

**San Antonio-area highway restrictions are exceptionally detailed.** Per the TxDMV San Antonio District Permit Restrictions document (updated through 3/17/2026), key constraints include an **11-foot maximum width** on segments of IH-35 between Binz Engleman Road and Eisenhauer Road, a **10-foot maximum width** on US-281 between LP 13 and North IH-410, and **no oversize permits at all** on certain IH-10/LP-1604 segments during weekends. Inside Loop 410, no oversize loads are permitted on any highway **unless the vehicle has an origin or destination there** (Restriction #19802). The San Antonio District contact for permit questions is SAT.OSOW.Permits@TxDOT.gov, with specific contacts at (210) 615-5912 and (210) 615-5866.

## 3. DPS escorts are not what most people expect

A widespread misconception holds that Texas DPS troopers escort manufactured homes. In reality, **Texas DPS does not operate a formal fee-based oversize load escort program.** The escorts required under Transportation Code §623.099 are **private civilian escort flag vehicles (pilot cars)**, not law enforcement vehicles. This distinguishes Texas from states like Alabama, which runs state trooper escorts with published fee schedules.

Private escort vehicles must meet specific requirements under §623.099(c): gross vehicle weight between **1,000 and 10,000 pounds**, two flashing amber lights or one rotating amber beacon of at least 8 inches diameter, four **16-inch red square flags** mounted on the vehicle's corners, a "WIDE LOAD" sign with yellow background and black letters, and two-way radio communication with the permitted vehicle. Texas requires **pilot/escort vehicle operator (PEVO) certification** — the state accepts Washington State PEVO certification offered through the Evergreen Safety Council.

When law enforcement escorts are specifically required by a permit or local jurisdiction, they are typically arranged through **off-duty police officers** via private security companies. Market rates run approximately **$75–$150 per hour** per officer with 4-hour minimums. Companies like First Responder Protective Services (Texas DPS Private Security License #C10520601) provide this service in the San Antonio area. However, §623.099(f)–(g) contains strong preemption language: **counties and municipalities may not require escort arrangements beyond what state law specifies** for manufactured homes.

A key convoy provision allows two transportable sections of a multisection home (or two singlewide homes) towed within **1,000 feet** of each other to share escorts as if they were one unit (§623.099(d)). The towing vehicle must display four red or orange 16-inch flags on the front (§623.100), and each manufactured home must have a wiring harness providing rear taillights, brake lights, and turn signals (§623.102(b)). The TDHCA license or registration number must be affixed to the rear of the home in letters at least **8 inches high** (§623.094(b)).

## 4. Bexar County's surprisingly light regulatory hand

Unincorporated Bexar County operates under a **limited regulatory framework** for manufactured homes due to Texas Local Government Code Chapter 233, Subchapter C (§§233.061–233.067), which restricts county authority over single-family residential construction. For single-family manufactured homes (1–3 dwelling units), the county **cannot legally mandate building permits** — permitting is voluntary unless required by an HOA or a lending institution for a Certificate of Occupancy. Furthermore, **unincorporated Bexar County has no zoning**, meaning no zones prohibit manufactured homes and no county-imposed setback or lot size requirements exist (outside the City of San Antonio's Military Protection Area).

When permits are obtained voluntarily — or when commercial manufactured home parks (4+ units) are involved — the process runs through the **Bexar County Fire Marshal's Office (BCFMO)** and Public Works. A Building Permit Authorization (BPA) from Public Works at 1948 Probandt Street must be obtained first, followed by submission to the Fire Marshal at 9810 Southton Road. Applications are accepted **in person by appointment only** at (210) 335-0300 (option 2). There is **no online permitting portal** for Bexar County.

The fee schedule (effective January 1, 2024) sets **$750 flat** for a modular/mobile structure installation permit (commercial/public use), which includes plan review, installation inspection, and Certificate of Occupancy. Trade permits for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC run **$150 each** ($250 if not associated with an active building permit). Floodplain development permits cost **$50 per activity** through the Development Services Section of Public Works. State law gives the county **30 days** to review and approve or deny any voluntarily submitted building permit (§233.063).

On-site sewage facility (OSSF/septic) permits through Bexar County Environmental Services are mandatory when municipal sewer is unavailable — and critically, **no utility meters will be released for manufactured homes until the septic system is installed and permitted**. Contact Bexar County Environmental Services at (210) 335-6700 or via bexar.org/3218/On-Site-Sewage-Facilities.

## 5. San Antonio's floodplain rules add real complexity

The City of San Antonio's Development Services Department (DSD) at 1901 S. Alamo Street imposes the most detailed local requirements for manufactured home installation. Two separate permit applications exist: one for **private lot installation** and one for **placement in an established park**. Both are available through the **BuildSA online portal** at www.sa.gov/Directory/Departments/DSD/BuildSA and as PDFs at docsonline.sanantonio.gov.

The manufactured home installation permit fee is **$78 flat** (FY 2025 fee schedule, revised October 2025). Required submittals for private lot installation include a completed building permit application, a notarized tree application/affidavit, two sets of site plans matching the recorded plat, a **foundation plan stamped and sealed by a Texas Professional Engineer or Registered Architect**, floor plans, exterior elevations, and a window/door schedule. Separate trade permits are required for electrical, plumbing, mechanical, sewer/septic, and gas work. Inspections include foundation, framing, and final — all must be completed before utility release. Applications expire if no permit issues within **180 days**, with extensions available in 180-day increments.

**Zoning is the primary barrier** within San Antonio. The Unified Development Code (UDC) limits manufactured homes to three special districts: **"MH" Manufactured Housing District (§35-354)** for individual lots, **"MHC" Manufactured Housing Conventional District (§35-355)** for conventional subdivisions, and **"MHP" Manufactured Housing Park District (§35-356)** for parks. Manufactured homes are generally **prohibited in standard residential districts** (R-4, R-5, R-6, etc.). Properties must front on a paved city street per UDC §35-506(d)(9)C.3. Zoning verification is available at (210) 207-1111.

San Antonio's floodplain exposure is substantial, governed by **UDC Appendix F ("Floodplains — Areas of Special Flood Hazard")**. Key provisions under §35-F142 require that the lowest floor of any new residential structure — including manufactured homes — be elevated **one foot above the base flood elevation (BFE)**. In AO/AH zones (§35-F143), the elevation must be one foot above the regulatory floodplain, one foot above the highest adjacent grade, or one foot above the FIRM depth number — whichever is highest. The 50% rule applies: improvements exceeding 50% of a structure's market value within a 10-year period trigger substantial-improvement requirements. A Floodplain Development Permit must be signed and sealed by a registered PE. Floodplain verification is handled by Transportation & Capital Improvements at (210) 207-8047.

## 6. Adjacent cities range from welcoming to restrictive

Six commonly involved jurisdictions surrounding San Antonio each present distinct regulatory environments for manufactured home placement.

**New Braunfels** (Comal County) is notably restrictive, limiting manufactured homes to just **three zoning districts: B-1, B-1A, and B-1B** under Section 144-4.2 of the Municipal Code. The city distinguishes between replacement homes in existing parks (no general contractor required, just a licensed installer) and new placements on private lots (general contractor required). A soil condition/geotechnical report is mandatory for all installations, and a separate utility application packet must be filed with New Braunfels Utilities at newconstruction@nbutexas.com. Permits are processed through ProjectDox; forms are available at newbraunfels.gov/3432/Forms-Applications. (Source: Manufactured Home Permits Information Bulletin, July 21, 2023.)

**Boerne** (Kendall County) presents a challenge for manufactured home placement — no specific manufactured home permitting pathway was identified in the city's ordinances, and the Historic District's aesthetic standards may effectively preclude placement in many areas. Residential building permit fees run **$0.38/sq ft for homesteads** and $0.53/sq ft for non-homesteads (fee schedule effective October 1, 2024). Properties in the ETJ fall under Kendall County jurisdiction. The city uses My Government Online (MGO) for permits at ci.boerne.tx.us/295/Permits, with the Permitting department reachable at (830) 248-1529.

**Seguin** (Guadalupe County) requires a **Limited Use Permit** for modular/manufactured homes, adding an extra approval layer beyond standard building permits. The city uses a square-footage-based fee structure compliant with Texas HB 852. The approved building final inspection serves as the Certificate of Occupancy for 1–2 family dwellings — no separate CO document is issued. The online portal is at eportal.seguintexas.gov; permits and inspections information is at seguintexas.gov/554/Permits-and-Inspections.

**Kyle** (Hays County) has the most explicit manufactured home moving provisions among these cities, including a specific permit fee for moving manufactured homes **in or out of the city** and police escort provisions when deemed necessary. Registered retailers and installers placing homes on dealer lots are exempt from the moving permit and inspection fees. Work begun without a permit triggers **double fees**. An ETJ inspection surcharge applies for properties outside city limits. The building department is at (512) 262-3911 with online permits at cityofkyle.com/building.

**San Marcos** (Hays County) maintains a dedicated **Mobile Home Permit Application** and a **2020 Mobile Home Permit Guide**, indicating a separate permitting track. The fee schedule was approved by Ordinance 2023-04, effective March 1, 2023, based on a cost-of-service study. The city uses character-district-based zoning that adds nuance to placement decisions. The permit center is at sanmarcostx.gov/1000/Permit-Center, using MGO Connect for online processing.

**Schertz** (spanning Guadalupe, Bexar, and Comal Counties) is distinctive for its **AICUZ (Air Installation Compatible Use Zone)** restrictions tied to Randolph Air Force Base, which impose unique land use constraints. The UDC allows nonconforming HUD-Code manufactured homes to be **replaced with another HUD-Code home** (per Ordinance 25-S-024). Initial plan review takes up to **5 business days**. The CityView Portal for online permits is at development.schertz.com/Portal/. Schertz offers Saturday office hours (9 AM–5 PM), unusual among these jurisdictions.

## 7. Interstate moves multiply the permit stack

A manufactured home crossing state lines requires **separate oversize permits from each state** — no centralized multi-state system exists. Each neighboring state has its own permit fees, dimensional limits, escort thresholds, and online portals.

**Louisiana (LaDOTD)** charges just **$10 per trip** for a mobile home oversize permit valid for 3 consecutive days, filed through the LaGeaux system at https://lageauxpm.dotd.la.gov/ or by phone at (800) 654-1433. Escorts are required for loads exceeding **12 feet wide and/or 90 feet long**. Loads wider than 12 feet are prohibited on Interstate through Shreveport, Monroe, Lake Charles, Baton Rouge, and New Orleans metro areas. A minimum **$100,000 insurance certificate** naming LaDOTD as holder is required for single trips ($250,000 for charge accounts), per Act 301 of the 2019 Regular Session. Mardi Gras restrictions prohibit travel in specific parishes during the celebration period. (Louisiana Administrative Code Title 73, Part I, Chapter 3, §I-303.)

**New Mexico (NMDOT/NMDPS)** charges **$25 per trip** for a 5-day permit, filed at https://truckpermits.dot.nm.gov/ or at ports of entry. A unique provision requires proof that **all property taxes on the manufactured home are paid** before a permit is issued for moves originating in New Mexico, with the county assessor notified of both origin and destination (N.M. Stat. Ann. §66-7-413). Maximum permitted width is an absolute **18 feet** with strict overhang limits. State Police escorts cost an additional **$300 per day** when required (loads over 20 feet wide). Self-issue permit books are available for **$250 per book of ten** for transporters/dealers. (NMAC 18.19.8, effective 2/12/2016, amended 6/1/2022.)

**Oklahoma (ODOT)** charges **$40 per single trip** for manufactured homes, matching Texas's fee (OAC §730:50-5-18, adopted September 1, 2023). Permits are filed at http://swpermitsok.com/ after establishing a company profile. Escort requirements trigger at **12 feet wide** (one escort) and **14 feet wide** (front and rear escorts). Loads **over 16 feet wide cannot use the Interstate/Defense Highway system**. Oklahoma requires specifically sized towing vehicles: a truck of at least 2 tons with rear duals for homes over 12 feet wide (OAC §595:30-3-19). Rush hour restrictions in Oklahoma City and Tulsa metro areas run 7 AM–9 AM and 3:30 PM–6:30 PM.

**Mexico border crossings** involve CBP documentation, HUD compliance, and customs brokerage. Homes imported into the U.S. must comply with **HUD Manufactured Home Construction and Safety Standards (24 CFR Part 3280)** and carry a HUD certification label — effectively requiring production in HUD-approved facilities. Exports require Electronic Export Information filing via AES for goods over $2,500. Eagle Pass's Camino Real International Bridge (Bridge II) offers a **25-foot-wide lane** suitable for manufactured home transport, while Laredo's World Trade Bridge handles the heaviest commercial volume. Mexican customs requires a Pedimento de Importación and engagement of a licensed customs broker. Under current trade policy, a **25% tariff** may apply to non-USMCA goods from Mexico per Executive Orders 14194 and 14198 (February 2025), though the tariff situation remains volatile.

All interstate moves also require **IRP (International Registration Plan) compliance** for commercial vehicles over 26,000 lbs GVW and **IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement)** quarterly fuel tax reporting. Without IRP registration, trip permits must be obtained for each state.

## 8. Texas carrier licensing hinges on existing TDHCA credentials

A critical finding of this analysis: **Texas does not issue a separate "transporter" license for manufactured home movers.** Under Occupations Code §1201.161(a), a licensed retailer or installer is **not required to obtain any additional permit, certificate, or license** to transport manufactured housing to the installation site, except for the TxDMV oversize permit under Transportation Code Chapter 623, Subchapter E. The TDHCA license types are manufacturer, retailer, broker, installer, and salesperson — no "transporter" category exists (§1201.003(17)).

The practical effect is that **the installer's existing TDHCA license and bond cover transportation activities**. The $25,000 installer surety bond (or $50,000 retailer bond) does not need supplementation for transport. However, the **$300,000 combined single limit liability insurance** requirement under Transportation Code §623.103 applies to **every vehicle towing a manufactured house**, including homeowner self-moves. This insurance threshold exceeds standard commercial auto minimums and represents a significant compliance requirement.

Transportation Code §623.094 limits permit eligibility to four categories: TDHCA-licensed manufacturers, retailers, or installers; registered motor carriers with USDOT numbers; homeowners named on the title who own or lease the towing vehicle; or persons authorized by the TDHCA director. TxDMV verifies USDOT numbers against FMCSA's MCMIS database for out-of-service orders before issuing any permit. Penalties for unauthorized transport are substantial: **$1,000 for a first offense** (Class C misdemeanor), escalating to $2,000 plus up to 30 days in jail for a second offense, and $5,000 plus up to one year in jail for subsequent violations (§623.104). Paying an unlicensed person to move a home carries a separate **$1,000 misdemeanor fine** (§623.105).

Section §1201.161(d) provides important preemption: **local governments may not require a separate permit, fee, bond, or insurance for transportation or installation** by a licensed retailer or installer, except for collection of actual costs incurred. This means municipal transport permits — beyond the state-level TxDMV permit — are generally preempted for TDHCA licensees.

## Conclusion: a regulatory stack that rewards preparation

The manufactured home transport and installation framework in San Antonio involves genuine regulatory complexity, but it follows a logical hierarchy. **TDHCA licensing forms the foundation** — without a valid installer or retailer license and the associated $25,000+ bond and $300,000 liability insurance, no legitimate transport can occur. **TxDMV's $40 single-trip permit through TxPROS** handles the highway authorization and routes around San Antonio's many corridor-specific width and height restrictions, particularly the tight constraints inside Loop 410. The escort system relies on **private pilot cars, not DPS troopers**, with thresholds more generous for manufactured homes (16 feet) than general oversize loads (14 feet).

Local permitting varies dramatically: unincorporated Bexar County effectively cannot require single-family manufactured home building permits, while the City of San Antonio restricts placement to three dedicated MH zoning districts and imposes rigorous floodplain elevation requirements. Adjacent cities add their own variations — New Braunfels limits MH to three zones, Kyle charges explicit moving fees with police escort provisions, and Schertz layers AICUZ military compatibility requirements. Interstate moves multiply the permit stack but at modest cost (Louisiana at just $10 per trip, New Mexico at $25, Oklahoma at $40).

The single most consequential finding for practitioners is the **state preemption principle**: both §623.099(g) (prohibiting local escort add-ons) and §1201.161(d) (prohibiting local transport licensing) establish that TDHCA-licensed operators with valid TxDMV permits operate under state authority that local jurisdictions cannot supplement with additional transport-layer requirements. Understanding this preemption — while still complying with local installation and zoning requirements at the destination — is the key to navigating the San Antonio manufactured home transport framework efficiently.
