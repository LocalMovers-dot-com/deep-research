# Manufactured home transport costs in the San Antonio metro, 2026

**Moving a manufactured home in the San Antonio–New Braunfels metro area costs between $5,000 and $35,000 depending on home type, distance, and service scope.** A single-wide local full-service move runs **$5,000–$8,000**, a double-wide **$10,000–$16,000**, and a triple-wide **$15,000–$25,000** — but those ranges exclude skirting, steps, utility hookups, and inspections. When every dollar a homeowner actually spends is tallied, realistic all-in costs climb to **$8,500–$13,000** for a single-wide, **$15,000–$22,000** for a double-wide, and **$22,000–$35,000** for a triple-wide on a sub-50-mile move. This analysis draws on TxDMV permit schedules, TDHCA fee rules, FMCSA carrier records, Census housing data, and pricing from ProMatcher, HomeAdvisor/Angi, HomeGuide, CostHelper, and multiple Texas-based transporters to produce the most granular cost picture available for this market.

---

## Transport-only versus full-service pricing for local moves under 50 miles

The critical distinction in manufactured home moving is between **transport-only** (tow truck hauls the structure from point A to point B) and **full-service** (disconnect, transport, reconnect, block, level, anchor, skirt, and finish). Most national cost guides conflate the two, producing confusingly wide ranges.

### Single-wide (14 × 56 to 16 × 80 ft)

| Metric | Transport-only | Full-service |
|---|---|---|
| Low estimate | $1,500 | $5,000 |
| High estimate | $5,000 | $8,000 |
| **Median** | $3,000 | **$6,500** |
| **Mean** | $3,200 | **$6,500** |

ProMatcher reports a San Antonio–area (ZIP 78284) transport-only average of **$2,463** with a range of $2,200–$2,726, though that data originates from July 2018 and understates current pricing by roughly 25–35%. HomeAdvisor's March 2025 national data centers the single-wide full-service average at **$6,000**, while Texas-specific 2025 firsthand reports on Quora place full-service single-wide moves at **$5,000–$8,000** within a 50-mile radius. CostHelper's 2024 data cites $3,000–$5,000 for full-service short-distance single-wides, but this appears to reflect lower-cost rural markets, not San Antonio.

### Double-wide (two 14–16 ft sections, 28 × 48 to 32 × 80 ft)

| Metric | Transport-only | Full-service |
|---|---|---|
| Low estimate | $3,000 | $10,000 |
| High estimate | $6,000 | $16,000 |
| **Median** | $4,500 | **$13,000** |
| **Mean** | $4,500 | **$13,500** |

Double-wides require transporting two sections on separate trucks (or in tandem), then reassembling the marriage line on-site. HomeAdvisor's national average is **$11,500**; Texas-specific data runs higher. A 2025 Quora user reported paying **$15,000** for a double-wide move within 50 miles in Texas (handling their own utility disconnect/reconnect), while another Texas user cited **$12,000–$18,000** as the going range. ProMatcher lists a San Antonio double-wide long-distance rate of **$2.50–$4.75 per mile** (transport-only, August 2022 data from Texas Statewide Mobile Home Transport, ZIP 78223).

### Triple-wide (three 14–16 ft sections)

| Metric | Transport-only | Full-service |
|---|---|---|
| Low estimate | $5,000 | $15,000 |
| High estimate | $10,000 | $25,000 |
| **Median** | $7,500 | **$20,000** |
| **Mean** | $7,500 | **$20,000** |

Triple-wides are uncommon and data is sparse. Angi's March 2026 update cites **$12,000–$25,000+** nationally. First Choice Home Center (yourfchc.com) quotes **$10,000–$14,000+** for full-service up to 60 miles. Three separate transport trips, three TxDMV permits, and two marriage-line assemblies make triple-wides roughly **50–70% more expensive** than double-wides.

## Long-distance pricing from 50 to 200+ miles

Long-distance moves layer incremental per-mile transport charges onto the same fixed setup costs. The tow truck rate runs **$4.00–$5.50 per loaded mile per section**, while pilot/escort cars (when required) add **$1.50–$2.00 per mile** each.

| Home type | 100-mile full-service | 200-mile full-service |
|---|---|---|
| Single-wide | $9,000–$16,000 | $13,000–$20,000 |
| Double-wide | $15,000–$24,000 | $20,000–$32,000 |
| Triple-wide | $22,000–$35,000 | $30,000–$45,000 |

The transport-only component for a 100-mile single-wide move is approximately **$4,000–$5,500** (versus $1,500–$3,000 for a 30-mile local move). For double-wides, the per-mile premium doubles because two sections travel independently. HomeGuide (2026) cites an all-in long-distance rate of **$6–$15 per mile**, which aligns with the blended rates calculated below.

### Pilot/escort vehicles

Texas has specific manufactured housing escort rules (TxDMV, accessed March 2026) that are more lenient than general oversize-load rules:

| Section width | Escorts required | Cost per mile (per escort) | Typical cost at 30 mi |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤16 ft (most single-wide sections) | **0** | $0 | $0 |
| 16.1–18 ft | **1** | $1.50–$2.00 | $45–$60 |
| >18 ft | **2** | $3.00–$4.00 combined | $90–$120 |

**Key finding:** Most single-wide and double-wide sections fall at or below 16 feet, meaning **no escort vehicle is required** under Texas manufactured housing rules — only rotating amber beacons on the home and tow vehicle. This saves $45–$120+ per trip versus states with stricter escort thresholds. Escort costs become material only on triple-wide sections exceeding 18 feet or when double-wides are moved as a single unit (requiring a House Move permit and two escorts).

## Per-mile blended rates decline sharply with distance

Because manufactured home moves carry heavy fixed costs (disconnection, setup, skirting, steps, permits), the blended per-mile rate drops dramatically as distance increases. The table below shows all-in cost divided by total miles.

| Distance | Single-wide $/mile | Double-wide $/mile | Triple-wide $/mile |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 miles (local) | **$320** | **$578** | **$797** |
| 50 miles | $220 | $400 | $560 |
| 100 miles | $145 | $230 | $340 |
| 200 miles | $95 | $155 | $220 |

At 30 miles, fixed costs dominate and the per-mile figure appears extremely high. By 200 miles, the incremental transport charges ($4–$5.50/mile per section) bring the blended rate down to a level more recognizable to the freight industry. **For budgeting purposes, the most useful blended rate is at 100 miles: roughly $145/mile for a single-wide, $230/mile for a double-wide, and $340/mile for a triple-wide, all-in.**

## Bexar County's manufactured housing stock is smaller than you'd expect

Census Bureau American Community Survey data (2019–2023 five-year estimates, via Point2Homes and Census Reporter) shows manufactured housing comprising just **3.1% of Bexar County's 828,959 housing units** — approximately **25,700 manufactured homes**. This is well below the Texas statewide average of **6.5%** and far below neighboring counties that form the SA metro's exurban ring.

| County | Total housing units | Manufactured homes (est.) | % of stock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bexar | 828,959 | ~25,700 | 3.1% |
| Comal | 101,207 | ~8,100 | 8.0% |
| Guadalupe | 146,107 | ~14,600 | 10.0% |
| **SA-NB MSA (3-county core)** | **~1,076,000** | **~48,400** | **~4.5%** |
| Texas (statewide) | 11,890,808 | ~772,900 | 6.5% |

Guadalupe County's **10% manufactured housing share** makes it the densest market for mobile home transport services in the metro. Bexar County's urbanized core suppresses its percentage, but the absolute count of ~25,700 units — plus an estimated **70 mobile home parks** (per MobileHome.net) — still generates substantial transport demand for relocations, park closures, and dealer deliveries. The MSA's total manufactured housing inventory of roughly 48,000–55,000 units (including smaller outlying counties with higher MH shares) supports a modest but sustained local moving industry.

## Conclusion: a fixed-cost-heavy market with thin carrier supply

Three findings stand out from this analysis. First, **fixed costs dwarf transport charges** in manufactured home moves under 50 miles — foundation work, skirting, steps, and utility reconnection account for 60–70% of a typical all-in local move cost, while the actual trucking is 15–25%. Homeowners who focus solely on the transport quote and neglect to budget for setup and finishing will be blindsided by a bill that doubles their expectations. Second, **the San Antonio carrier market is thin**: six SAFER-verified transporters with a combined fleet of roughly 25–30 power units serve a metro area with ~48,000 manufactured housing units. This supply constraint explains the 30–45 day booking lead times and leaves little room for competitive pricing pressure. Third, **SAWS impact fees are the wild card** — at $7,343–$11,528 for a new residential water/sewer connection (as of July 2024), they can single-handedly add more to the project cost than the transport itself. Homeowners moving to a lot with existing utility service avoid this entirely; those placing on raw land should treat it as the largest single line item in their budget.

The TxDMV permit structure is remarkably affordable — **$40 per section per trip** — and Texas's manufactured-housing-specific escort exemption (no escort required up to 16 feet wide) saves $100–$300 per move compared to states applying standard oversize-load escort rules. TDHCA installation fees ($75–$125) are similarly modest. The real costs in this market are labor and materials at the destination site, not regulatory compliance.
