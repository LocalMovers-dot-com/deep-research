# San Antonio mobile home mover market: fragmented, family-run, and ripe for comparison shopping

**The San Antonio manufactured home transport market is dominated by small, family-owned operators running one to five trucks, with a single mid-size player — Alamo Transport — controlling the only fleet of meaningful scale.** This fragmentation gives consumers real pricing leverage, but it also means due diligence on licensing and insurance is critical. Across Bexar County and the five surrounding counties (Comal, Guadalupe, Medina, Kendall, Wilson), an estimated **20–45 standalone installer/transport businesses** compete for roughly **3,500 annual manufactured home installations**, according to proportional analysis of TDHCA FY2023 data. Full-service moves within 50 miles typically run **$5,000–$8,000 for a single-wide** and **$12,000–$18,000 for a double-wide**, though transport-only pricing can drop as low as $1,000–$3,500 for a single-wide local haul. The pending passage of SB 785 (effective September 1, 2026) will expand placement zones statewide, likely boosting demand for transport services in the near term.

## Three pricing tiers define the San Antonio market

### Budget tier: transport-only operators

Budget operators provide tow-and-go service — they hitch the home to a toter, pull the necessary TxDMV permits, and deliver it to the new site. **No disconnection, no setup, no leveling, no utility hookup.** This is the right choice for consumers who have a separate installer lined up or are moving a home to temporary storage.

**Typical pricing:** Single-wide transport only within 50 miles runs **$1,000–$3,500**; double-wide runs **$2,000–$5,000** (each half hauled separately). Per-mile rates for longer hauls land at **$5–$10 per mile** for the towing vehicle, plus **$1.50–$3.50 per mile** for a required escort vehicle on wider loads.

**Who operates here:** Five Star Mobile Home Transporting (Elmendorf, 1 unit), Solis Manufactured Homes (San Antonio, 1 unit), and some of the smaller unregistered operators advertising on Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist. Melinda's Transporting and the Trevino Transportation entities also compete at the budget level for straightforward local hauls.

**Tradeoffs:** Lower cost comes with higher consumer responsibility. The homeowner must separately arrange disconnection of utilities, hire a TDHCA-licensed installer for setup at the destination, manage permit coordination, and handle any site preparation. Scheduling flexibility may be limited with single-truck operators, and insurance coverage may be minimal. There is also a higher risk of encountering unlicensed operators at this price point.

### Standard tier: full-service local movers

Standard-tier operators handle the complete cycle — utility disconnection, transport with permits and escorts, placement, blocking/leveling/anchoring, skirting, and basic utility reconnection. This is the **typical consumer purchase** for park-to-park or park-to-private-land relocations.

**Typical pricing:** Full-service single-wide moves within 50 miles run **$5,000–$8,000**; double-wide moves run **$12,000–$18,000**. This includes TxDMV single-trip permits ($40 each; two needed for a double-wide), escort vehicles, blocking and leveling, tie-downs, and basic utility hookup.

**Who operates here:** Martinez Mobile Homes, Texas Elite Mobile Home Transport, RH Mobile Home Installers, R&H Transport Services, and San Antonio Mobile Home Transport all compete in the standard tier. These companies hold TDHCA installer licenses (or claim equivalent licensing), carry cargo insurance, and offer end-to-end project management.

**Typical customer:** A homeowner relocating a used single-wide or double-wide from one mobile home park to another, or from a park to private land within the San Antonio metro or surrounding counties. Military families at JBSA seeking affordable housing represent a meaningful customer segment, particularly during PCS season (May–September).

### Premium tier: complex and interstate moves

Premium operators handle the jobs that smaller companies either cannot or will not take — **triple-wide homes, Hill Country placements requiring specialized routing through narrow roads and elevation changes, ranch land access on unpaved roads, interstate transport requiring multi-state permits, and flood zone compliance** in FEMA-designated areas of Bexar County.

**Typical pricing:** Triple-wide full-service moves start at **$15,000 and can exceed $25,000**. Hill Country placements add **$2,000–$5,000** to standard rates due to routing complexity, additional escort requirements, and slower transport speeds on winding FM roads. Interstate moves from San Antonio to destinations 500+ miles away can run **$15,000–$25,000+** for a double-wide including multi-state permits.

**Who operates here:** **Alamo Transport** is the primary local premium operator, with 18 power units and the capacity to handle complex multi-section moves and site development. **Bennett Truck Transport** dominates interstate and new-home delivery. For Hill Country placements specifically, **Martinez Mobile Homes** has a strong reputation for navigating tight-access situations based on four decades of local experience. GoreSet Mobile Home Movers (Kerrville, BBB A+) also serves the Hill Country corridor from the west.

**Why they charge more:** Premium pricing reflects higher insurance requirements for high-value loads, multi-escort configurations (loads over 18 feet wide require front and rear escorts), complex permit routing through TxPROS, specialized site development (land clearing, grading, foundation work for FHA compliance), and the operational risk of moving oversized loads on challenging terrain.

### National carriers versus local operators

Bennett Truck Transport and broker networks (We Will Transport It, Best Mobile Home Mover, Heavy Haulers) primarily serve **new-home delivery from factories to dealer lots or consumer sites**, not used-home relocations. Bennett's pricing is negotiated per project and is generally **15–30% higher** than local operators for comparable moves due to overhead, insurance standards, and safety compliance costs. However, Bennett offers capabilities that no local operator can match: interstate authority across 48 states, a Satisfactory FMCSA safety rating, disaster-response mobilization, and modular building transport. For consumers buying a new manufactured home from a San Antonio-area dealer, the delivery cost is typically **bundled into the purchase price at $2,000–$14,000**, with the dealer contracting directly with Bennett or another national carrier.

## Market concentration favors consumers — but verification is essential

The San Antonio manufactured home transport market is **highly fragmented** with low barriers to entry. Based on TDHCA FY2023 data, approximately **210 standalone installer licensees** operate statewide, with an estimated **20–45 serving the six-county San Antonio metro** (Bexar, Comal, Guadalupe, Medina, Kendall, Wilson). When retailer/installer combination licenses are included, the total entities authorized to transport and install manufactured homes in the region rises to an estimated **100–200+**. BBB.org lists **21 manufactured home transport companies** near San Antonio, which likely represents only 30–40% of active operators given that many small businesses skip BBB registration.

**No single operator dominates.** Alamo Transport's 18-unit fleet is the largest, but it primarily serves the Alamo Homes dealer pipeline rather than the open consumer market. Most operators run **1–6 trucks**. This fragmentation creates genuine **pricing leverage for consumers** — obtaining three to five quotes is both feasible and advisable, as price variation of **30–50%** between operators for the same move is common.

The military presence at **Joint Base San Antonio** generates steady housing demand. With site-built homes averaging ~$410,000 in the metro versus ~$116,000 for manufactured homes, the affordability gap drives military families toward manufactured housing, particularly in surrounding communities like Schertz, Cibolo, Seguin, and Floresville. PCS season (May–September) creates predictable demand spikes that can push scheduling lead times from the standard **30–45 days** to **6–8 weeks** and add a seasonal premium.

**Regulatory tailwind:** SB 785, effective September 1, 2026, will expand where manufactured homes can legally be placed across Texas by removing many local functional bans. This is expected to increase transport demand in suburban and exurban areas around San Antonio, potentially tightening supply and firming prices in the near term.

## How to verify a San Antonio transporter before signing a contract

A San Antonio homeowner should complete four verification steps before hiring any manufactured home transport company. Each step takes 5–15 minutes online.

**Step 1 — Verify TDHCA installer license.** Navigate to the TDHCA license lookup tool at **https://mhweb.tdhca.state.tx.us/mhweb/license_view.jsp** (last updated February 27, 2026). Search by company name or license number. Texas does not issue a separate "transporter" license — the **Installer license** covers both transport and installation. Confirm the license status shows "Active" and note the expiration date. All installers must carry a **$25,000 surety bond**. If the company holds a Retailer/Installer (R/I) license, the bond is $50,000.

**Step 2 — Verify FMCSA operating authority and safety record.** Go to the FMCSA SAFER Company Snapshot at **https://safer.fmcsa.dot.gov/CompanySnapshot.aspx**. Search by USDOT number (ask the company for this number — a legitimate operator will provide it immediately). Confirm the carrier's status shows "ACTIVE" with no out-of-service orders. Check the 24-month inspection and crash history. For **interstate moves only**, verify that the company holds active operating authority (MC number) and has filed proof of **$750,000 minimum public liability insurance** via FMCSA's Licensing & Insurance page at **https://li-public.fmcsa.dot.gov**. Note: most San Antonio-area operators are intrastate-only and will show "NOT AUTHORIZED" for interstate authority — this is normal if they only operate within Texas.

**Step 3 — Verify TxDMV oversize-load permit authority.** The company must obtain a **TxDMV single-trip manufactured housing permit** ($40 per trip; each half of a double-wide requires a separate permit) through the TxPROS system at **https://txpros.txdmv.gov**. Ask to see the permit before the scheduled move date. The permit specifies the approved route, curfew times, and required escort configuration:

- Loads **≤16 feet wide**: no escort required
- Loads **>16 to ≤18 feet wide**: one escort (front on two-lane roads, rear on four-lane)
- Loads **>18 feet wide**: two escorts (front and rear)

Transport is restricted to **daylight hours only** (half-hour before sunrise to half-hour after sunset) and prohibited on all six federal holidays.

**Step 4 — Verify insurance coverage.** Request a current certificate of insurance (COI) showing:

- **Commercial auto/trucking liability**: minimum $500,000 for intrastate carriers (Texas Transportation Code Ch. 643); $750,000 for interstate
- **Cargo insurance**: FMCSA minimums are $5,000 per vehicle / $10,000 per occurrence, but industry practice for manufactured home transport should be **$100,000+ cargo coverage** to protect the home's value in transit
- **Active surety bond**: $25,000 (installer) or $50,000 (retailer/installer) filed with TDHCA

**Red flags to watch for:** Any company that cannot provide a USDOT number, refuses to show a TDHCA license number, quotes suspiciously low prices (under $2,000 for a full-service local move), requests full payment upfront, or has a BBB rating of F. One San Antonio operator — **Lone Star Mobile Home & Residential** (BBB rating: F, owner Nathan Supinger) — has two unanswered BBB complaints and two licensing alerts for failure to maintain required competency licensing. Consumers should avoid this company.

**Where to file complaints:** TDHCA Manufactured Housing Division at **(877) 313-3023** or installations@tdhca.state.tx.us for licensing and installation issues; TxDMV at **(800) 299-1700** for transport permit violations; FMCSA at **1-888-DOT-SAFT** for interstate safety concerns.

## Conclusion: a buyer's market with hidden risks

San Antonio's manufactured home transport market offers consumers meaningful pricing power through its fragmented competitive structure — **getting three to five quotes can easily save $2,000–$5,000** on a typical move. The market's most reliable operators cluster in the standard tier, led by long-established family businesses like Martinez Mobile Homes (43 years) and mid-size operations like Alamo Transport (18 trucks). For consumers needing interstate capability or new-home delivery, Bennett Truck Transport remains the only carrier with the scale, safety record, and multi-state authority to handle those jobs.

The coming expansion of manufactured home placement zones under SB 785 will likely increase demand and could tighten the market in late 2026 and beyond. Consumers planning moves should book **6–8 weeks ahead during summer PCS season** and **30–45 days** during off-peak months. The single most important safeguard is verification: confirming a transporter's TDHCA installer license, active USDOT registration, valid TxDMV permits, and adequate insurance takes less than an hour and eliminates the most serious risks in what remains a lightly regulated, cash-heavy industry.
