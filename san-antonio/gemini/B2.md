San Antonio Mobile Home Transport Regulatory & Permitting Framework — 2026 Market Analysis
Executive Summary

The transportation, routing, and installation of manufactured housing within the San Antonio Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA) constitutes a highly specialized and heavily regulated logistical ecosystem. As of 2026, motor carriers, logistics brokers, and installation contractors must navigate an intricate, multi-layered regulatory architecture that spans federal mandates, state-level statutory codes, and highly fragmented municipal ordinances. Moving a structure that frequently exceeds 100 feet in length and 80,000 pounds in gross vehicle weight across public right-of-ways requires precise synchronization of oversize/overweight (OS/OW) permitting, law enforcement escorts, structural engineering compliance, and localized land-use zoning.

This exhaustive market analysis maps the complete regulatory and permitting framework governing the movement of HUD-code manufactured homes and mobile homes into, out of, and within the San Antonio region. The report dissects the compliance mandates enforced by the Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs (TDHCA), the routing optimization and permit fee schedules under the Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT) and the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (TxDMV), and the dynamic pilot car and law enforcement escort protocols under the Department of Public Safety (DPS). Furthermore, this analysis details the localized zoning, building permit, and inspection requirements of Bexar County, the City of San Antonio, and rapidly expanding adjacent municipalities. Finally, the report addresses the complexities of interstate transit across neighboring state lines and international border crossings, alongside the rigorous statutory capitalization, bonding, and insurance minimums required for carriers operating within this specialized freight sector.

1. State Regulatory Architecture: TDHCA Compliance and Licensing

The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs (TDHCA), operating through its Manufactured Housing Division (MHD), serves as the primary regulatory authority overseeing the manufacturing, retailing, brokering, and installation of manufactured homes. This authority is derived from the Texas Manufactured Housing Standards Act, codified under Chapter 1201 of the Texas Occupations Code, and the administrative regulations detailed in the Texas Administrative Code (TAC), Title 10, Part 1, Chapter 80. The state's regulatory philosophy decouples consumer protection from standard freight logistics, treating the delivery and setup of a manufactured home as a specialized structural engineering activity rather than mere cargo transportation.   

1.1 Installer Licensing Requirements and Educational Mandates

To legally transport, place, block, anchor, or re-level a manufactured home in the State of Texas, the operating entity must hold a valid Installer (I) license issued by the TDHCA. This requirement applies uniformly, meaning that even a motor carrier whose primary business is heavy-haul trucking must secure an Installer license if their contract includes the physical placement and stabilization of the home at the destination site.   

The statutory prerequisites for securing and maintaining a TDHCA Installer license in the 2026 framework are stringent and require significant upfront investment in education and capitalization:

Educational Coursework and Examinations: All applicants must successfully complete an 8-hour Core Licensing Education Class (LEC), which focuses on state law and consumer protection regulations. This core class carries a mandatory assessment fee of $150 per attendee. Following the core curriculum, prospective installers must complete an additional 4-hour Installer Licensing Education Course, which carries an additional fee of $50.   

Background and Application Processing: The submission of a formal license application requires fingerprinting for a comprehensive criminal history background check, accompanied by the provision of formal business formation documents, such as an Assumed Name Certificate filed with the County Clerk or Articles of Incorporation filed with the Texas Secretary of State.   

Provisional Licensing Rules: Under the current administrative framework, applicants may receive a provisional license to begin operations; however, they are strictly required to complete the necessary coursework and pass the state licensing exam administered by PSI Exams within 90 days of the license issuance date. Failure to meet this 90-day deadline results in the immediate suspension of the provisional license, prohibiting the entity from engaging in any installation or retail activities.   

The fee structure for TDHCA licensing is established under 10 TAC § 80.3 and is strictly enforced. Standard licenses are valid for a two-year (biennial) period.

1.2 Homeowner Verification and the TDHCA Interactive Portal

The TDHCA maintains robust transparency mechanisms designed to empower consumers and local municipalities to verify the credentials of installers and transporters. Homeowners, park managers, and municipal code enforcers can access the TDHCA's Manufactured Housing Division License Holder database via the "TDHCA Interactive" online portal.   

This centralized database allows users to query active license holder records, verify the status and standing of an installer's surety bond, and review historical installation and inspection records for specific entities. Furthermore, the portal provides access to the Vacancy Clearinghouse, home ownership records, and records of tax liens, ensuring that homeowners can verify that their contracted installer is legally authorized to perform structural work in Texas.   

1.3 Installation Acceptance Obligations, Warranties, and Inspection Fees

Under 10 TAC § 80.3, the state mandates rigorous post-transit reporting to ensure that the structural integrity of the home was not compromised during the move and that the setup complies with regional wind zone and soil bearing capacity regulations. These reporting mechanisms create a tightly controlled logistical timeframe that requires precise coordination between the transport carrier and the onsite installation crew.   

Notice of Installation (Form T): The licensed installer is legally required to submit a Notice of Installation (commonly referred to as Form T) to the TDHCA no later than seven (7) days after the physical installation is completed. For new installers operating under a provisional license, this reporting window is aggressively compressed to just three (3) days. The prompt filing of this notice is critical because the state must inspect the home's foundation, tie-downs, and anchoring systems before the structure is permanently enclosed by cosmetic skirting.   

Online Submissions: To streamline this process, the TDHCA allows installers to submit and pay for Form T notices through their online portal. This digital integration eliminates the delays associated with hard-copy mailings, reducing the likelihood that a home becomes inaccessible to state inspectors before the skirt is applied.   

Reporting and Seal Fees: The submission of Form T requires a mandatory reporting fee of $75 for a single-section manufactured home, with an additional $25 assessed for each subsequent section (e.g., $100 for a double-wide, $125 for a triple-wide). Furthermore, any used HUD-Code manufactured home (post-1976) or mobile home (pre-1976) that lacks a federally affixed HUD label must obtain a Texas Seal for identification and titling purposes. The fee for the issuance of a Texas Seal is $35 per section.   

Habitability and Consumer Protection Inspections: In the event of a dispute regarding the structural integrity, leveling, or habitability of the delivered home, a consumer, retailer, or manufacturer may request a formal home inspection from the TDHCA, which carries a $150 fee.   

Joint Warranty Obligations: The installer is legally bound to provide the homeowner with a written warranty guaranteeing that the installation conforms to all state generic standards (10 TAC §§ 80.22-80.25) or the manufacturer's specific DAPIA-approved (Design Approval Primary Inspection Agency) installation instructions. Section 1201.352 of the Texas Occupations Code dictates that the retailer of a new HUD-code home is ultimately responsible for the installation warranty at the initial homesite. However, if the retailer subcontracts the physical delivery and setup to an independent licensed installer, both the retailer and the installer bear joint and several liability for the performance of the warranty. This joint liability structure incentivizes retailers to thoroughly vet the safety records and bond standings of their transport partners.   

2. TxDOT & TxDMV: Oversize/Overweight Load Permitting Logistics

While the TDHCA regulates the commercial licensing and structural integrity of the installer, the physical transportation of the manufactured home over public right-of-ways falls squarely under the jurisdiction of the Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT) and the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (TxDMV). Because manufactured homes inherently exceed standard commercial freight dimensions (which typically cap at 8 feet 6 inches in width and 53 feet in trailer length), they require specialized Oversize/Overweight (OS/OW) permits.   

2.1 The TxPROS Permitting System and Fee Schedules

All routing, clearance checks, and permitting for OS/OW loads in Texas are processed through the Texas Permitting & Routing Optimization System (TxPROS). This highly sophisticated GIS-based system evaluates current highway geometrics, real-time construction closures, bridge weight tolerances, and municipal curfews to generate legally binding, turn-by-turn transit routes. Carriers are legally bound to follow the exact route generated by TxPROS; deviating from the specified path constitutes a severe violation of the permit.   

Permit classifications and 2026 fee structures for manufactured housing are strictly delineated by the frequency of movement and the nature of the load:

Manufactured Housing Single-Trip Permit: This is the most common permit utilized by carriers. It is issued for a single, continuous movement of a manufactured home on a returnable undercarriage or temporary chassis from a specific point of origin to a specific destination. The permit is valid for a maximum of five (5) days. The base fee for this permit is $40. When processed through the mandatory Texas.gov payment portal, a service charge of $0.25 plus 2.25% is added, bringing the effective total cost to approximately $41.16 per trip. Crucially, each section of a multi-section home (e.g., each half of a double-wide) requires its own distinct permit.   

Annual Manufactured Housing Permit: Designed primarily for high-volume manufacturers and large-scale retailers, this permit allows the continuous movement of newly manufactured homes from a manufacturing facility to a temporary storage location. However, this permit is highly restricted geographically; it is only valid for transit distances that do not exceed a 20-mile radius from the point of manufacture. The fee for the Annual Manufactured Housing Permit is $1,500 ($1,534.01 with online service charges).   

General Single-Trip Permit (Frames and Superloads): TxDOT classifies the returnable undercarriage, temporary chassis systems, and stacked manufactured housing frames as component parts, not as housing units. Therefore, hauling a stack of empty frames back to a factory requires a standard General Single-Trip Permit, which costs $60 ($61.61 with fees).   

Furthermore, if a highly customized modular unit or heavily reinforced manufactured home exceeds the state's legal gross axle weight limits (80,000 pounds), the carrier must obtain a General Single-Trip permit and pay additional Highway Maintenance Fees based on a sliding scale of the vehicle's total gross weight.

2.2 Dimensional Limits and Route Inspection Certifications

Standard TxPROS automated routing applies seamlessly to manufactured homes that fall within predictable oversize limits. However, extreme dimensions trigger manual engineering reviews and heightened scrutiny. If a manufactured home, modular building, or house move exceeds 20 feet in width, 18 feet 11 inches in height, or 125 feet in length, the carrier is legally required to execute and file a Route Inspection Certification prior to the issuance of the permit.   

This certification mandates that a representative of the transport company physically drive the entire proposed route prior to the move to verify vertical and horizontal clearances, identify unmapped overhead utility obstructions, and ensure that the turning radius of the load will not force the vehicle to leave the paved surface of the roadway. The carrier must then notify TxDMV in writing whether the vehicle and load can safely negotiate the proposed route; if any section is unacceptable, the carrier is responsible for providing an alternate route.   

2.3 San Antonio Metro Area Curfews and Highway Restrictions

The San Antonio highway network presents significant operational friction for manufactured home transporters. Characterized by heavy commuter traffic, complex interchanges, and ongoing infrastructure expansion projects, TxDOT enforces strict curfews and routing blackouts within the TxDOT San Antonio District to mitigate safety hazards and traffic congestion.   

Urban Core Commuter Curfews: To prevent massive traffic bottlenecks during rush hour, TxDOT explicitly prohibits the movement of any permitted oversize loads on or inside the Loop 1604 perimeter during the peak transit hours of 7:00 AM to 9:00 AM, and 4:00 PM to 6:00 PM, Monday through Friday. This effectively forces carriers to either transit through the city mid-day or stage their loads at the county lines until the curfew lifts.   

Weekend Blackouts on IH-35: Specifically at the critical junction of Interstate 35 and East Loop 1604, TxDOT mandates that no oversize permits are valid for maneuvers across IH-35 from 9:00 PM on Friday through 5:00 AM on Monday. This restriction essentially halts weekend cross-city transport on the region's primary north-south artery, requiring carriers to complete deliveries by Friday afternoon or wait until Monday morning.   

Infrastructure-Specific Restrictions (Bandera/Kerr Counties): The topography and aging infrastructure of the Hill Country northwest of San Antonio create unique hazards. For example, on State Highway 16 (SH 16) in Bandera County, any load exceeding 17 feet in height is heavily restricted. The mover must utilize a TxDOT-approved signal contractor to physically disconnect, temporarily raise, and reconnect any overhead mast arms or span wire assemblies along the route. The carrier must contact the San Antonio District Signal Shop (TransGuide) prior to movement to arrange this, and all related costs are borne by the transporter. Furthermore, loads over 11 feet wide or 16 feet 6 inches high on portions of SH 173/SH 16 cannot travel in a convoy and must notify the Bandera County Maintenance Office four hours prior to entry.   

Universal Holiday Blackouts: Beyond localized curfews, the movement of all manufactured housing is universally prohibited across the State of Texas on major national holidays. These blackout dates include New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day, and Christmas Day. TxDMV permit offices are also closed on these dates, meaning carriers cannot amend routes or resolve permit issues during these periods.   

3. DPS and Law Enforcement Escort Requirements

Because manufactured homes command such a massive physical footprint on the roadway, the Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS), in coordination with local law enforcement agencies and TxDMV, dictates the deployment of pilot cars (escort flag vehicles) and sworn police escorts. These vehicles are critical for warning the traveling public, managing traffic flow around tight turns, and measuring overhead clearances.

They must be equipped with two flashing amber lights or one rotating amber beacon (minimum 8 inches in diameter) mounted on the roof, 16-inch red flags mounted on the four corners of the vehicle, and two-way radio communication with the transport truck. Furthermore, the vehicle must display MUTCD-compliant "OVERSIZE LOAD" or "WIDE LOAD" signs featuring black lettering (8 to 10 inches high) on a yellow background.

3.2 Mandatory Law Enforcement Escorts and Bexar County Fees

While civilian pilot cars satisfy the baseline TxDMV requirements, specific operational hazards and localized municipal constraints frequently trigger the need for mandatory law enforcement escorts. TxDMV explicitly states that law enforcement escorts may be unilaterally required if a permitted vehicle must travel over the center line of a narrow bridge, make turning movements that force the load into opposing traffic lanes, reduce speed significantly to cross under a low overhead obstruction, or otherwise create an abnormal and unusual traffic flow pattern.   

In the San Antonio metro and surrounding Hill Country counties, specific routes necessitate sworn peace officers. For example, traveling on SH 16 in Bandera County with a height exceeding 17 feet requires mandatory law enforcement escorts, and the carrier must arrange this in advance by calling local authorities. Similarly, the City of Kerrville requires law enforcement escorts for any load over 14 feet wide, 14 feet high, or 110 feet long.   

Bexar County Sheriff and Constable Fees:
Coordinating sworn law enforcement escorts adds a layer of administrative complexity and cost. The Bexar County 2026 Civil Process Fee Schedule dictates standard transit and writ service fees (e.g., $117 for eviction citations, $157 for property attachment, and up to $884 for out-of-county mental health transports). For off-duty traffic control and oversize load escorts, the Bexar County Sheriff's Office typically establishes contracts based on an hourly rate. The standard fee schedule notes that specialized enforcement actions generally incur an additional $50.00 per hour, per deputy, after an initial 2-hour minimum engagement. Comparative regional data (such as the fee structure in the neighboring City of Kyle) establishes that municipal police escorts are typically billed at 1.5 times the hourly cost of the officer (including wages and benefits) plus the cost of the patrol vehicle, calculated for the estimated duration of the move.   

4. Bexar County Setup Requirements, Permits, and Inspections

Once the manufactured home clears the highway network and reaches its destination within the San Antonio MSA, the regulatory framework abruptly shifts from transportation logistics to municipal land use, zoning, and building code compliance. The jurisdictional authority depends entirely on whether the destination parcel sits within the incorporated City of San Antonio or in unincorporated Bexar County.

4.1 Unincorporated Bexar County Regulations

The Bexar County Fire Marshal's Office (BCFMO) and the Bexar County Public Works Department oversee development and structural placements in the unincorporated areas of the county. The county operates under a nuanced framework that heavily distinguishes between residential homes and commercial structures.   

Residential Exemptions and Permit Verification: Under Texas state law, Bexar County lacks the statutory authority to regulate purely residential single-family construction, conduct residential structural inspections, or issue residential Certificates of Occupancy. Consequently, placing a standard single-family manufactured home on a private rural lot does not require a traditional building permit or structural review from the county. However, the homeowner or installer must submit a Permit Verification (PV) Form to the Bexar County Public Works Department prior to setup. This free administrative review ensures that the placement does not violate the county's Stormwater Quality regulations, Floodplain Development rules, or the Military Lighting Court Order (which regulates exterior lighting within five miles of a military installation).   

Commercial and Public Access Structures: If the manufactured structure is classified as modular or mobile for commercial or public access use (e.g., a construction field office, a temporary sales center, or a classroom), the county's authority is absolute. The applicant must secure a formal MOD Permit. The fee for the MOD permit is $750, which covers the plan review of the structure, site inspections after installation to ensure compliance with the adopted Fire Code, and the issuance of a Certificate of Occupancy. If re-inspections are required due to code failures or if the approved plans are not on the job site during an inspection, a $100 penalty fee applies per visit.   

Environmental and Septic Permitting: While Bexar County does not inspect the structural framing of a residential manufactured home, the Environmental Services department strictly regulates the installation of On-Site Sewage Facilities (OSSF/Septic systems). The home cannot be legally occupied until the septic permits are approved and the system is inspected by county sanitarians.   

5. City of San Antonio: DSD Permits, Floodplains, and Zoning

Within the city limits, the San Antonio Development Services Department (DSD) enforces strict adherence to the 2015 International Residential Code (IRC) and the city's Unified Development Code (UDC). The installation of a manufactured home inside the city is treated with the same rigorous scrutiny as a site-built home.   

5.1 Zoning Strictures and Legislative Preemption (SB 785)

Manufactured homes are geographically restricted within San Antonio. They may only be legally installed on lots that are specifically zoned for Manufactured Housing (MH), Manufactured Housing Camp (MHC), or Single-Family with a Specific Use Authorization (CD or S). Furthermore, under UDC § 35-506, the property must front a paved city street; if it fronts an unpaved road, the owner is financially responsible for paving their half of the street right-of-way at their own cost prior to installation.   

It is critical to note that the regulatory landscape for zoning is shifting. The Texas 89th Legislature passed SB 785 (effective September 1, 2026), which preempts municipalities from selectively weaponizing Specific Use Permits to block manufactured housing. Under the new law, if a city requires a specific use permit for a manufactured home, it must require the exact same permit for all other forms of residential property in that same zoning classification. This effectively forces cities like San Antonio to designate areas on their zoning maps where manufactured homes are allowed "by right," dismantling decades of exclusionary zoning tactics.   

5.2 Floodplain Management and the "50% Rule"

Placements in or near a designated floodplain require a Floodplain Development Permit (FPDP) governed by the UDC and FEMA's 44 CFR 59.1. San Antonio strictly enforces the "50% rule," meaning that over a rolling 10-year period, the cumulative dollar amount of improvements made to a property (including the value of placing a new manufactured home) cannot exceed 50% of the pre-improvement market value of the structure/property. Exceeding this threshold triggers severe elevation requirements, mandating that the home be elevated above the base flood elevation (BFE) and structurally flood-proofed, which dramatically increases installation costs.   

5.3 Building and Trade Permit Fees

To execute an installation on a private lot in San Antonio, the applicant must submit a comprehensive submittal package. This package must include a site plan to scale, exterior elevations, and a foundation plan stamped and sealed by a Texas-licensed professional engineer, demonstrating compliance with the 2015 IRC.   

Base Installation Permit: The overarching Manufactured Home Installation Permit carries a base fee of $250, plus a $1.00 state surcharge.   

Trade Permits: Because the home must be connected to municipal utilities, independent licensed trade contractors must pull separate permits for their specific scopes of work. Typical 2026 trade fees include a $50 minimum for plumbing (plus $5 per fixture), $75 per mechanical/HVAC unit, and site inspection fees of $45 for residential lots.   

Utility Release Coordination: The DSD will not issue a utility release to CPS Energy or the San Antonio Water System (SAWS) until the foundation, framing, and all individual trade inspections (electrical, plumbing, mechanical, sewer/septic) have successfully passed.   

6. Adjacent Metro Jurisdictions: A Comparative Map

The geographic sprawl of the San Antonio MSA means carriers frequently transport homes into neighboring boomtowns along the IH-35 and IH-10 corridors. Each municipality maintains idiosyncratic zoning ordinances, aesthetic standards, and fee structures that deeply complicate regional logistics.

6.1 New Braunfels (Comal/Guadalupe Counties)

New Braunfels recently streamlined its ordinance for manufactured home parks to reduce administrative friction. As of 2023, a homeowner replacing an older unit within a pre-existing, platted mobile home park does not need to hire a registered General Contractor. However, the placement of a new manufactured home on a private residential lot still triggers the full General Contractor requirement. All permit applications, plans, and documents must be routed electronically through the city's ProjectDox Electronic Plan Review (EPR) software.   

6.2 Boerne (Kendall County)

Boerne utilizes its Unified Development Code (UDC) to enforce incredibly strict aesthetic and structural standards, effectively acting as an exclusionary barrier to older or smaller manufactured homes.

Dimensional Minimums: Manufactured homes must have a minimum horizontal dimension of 24 feet, essentially banning the placement of single-wide trailers in most residential zones.   

Structural Mandates: The city requires that all towing axles, hitches, and wheel assemblies be permanently removed at the time of placement. The home must be affixed to a permanent concrete foundation (concrete slabs or poured piers); the use of traditional concrete blocks is strictly prohibited. Furthermore, the space between the ground and the floor must be enclosed with non-degradable skirting that is compatible in color, style, and texture with the home's exterior.   

Fees: Building permits are assessed based on square footage at a rate of $0.38 per square foot (up to 5,000 sq. ft.), and a separate $84 building moving permit is required to bring the structure into the city limits.   

6.3 Seguin (Guadalupe County)

Seguin differentiates sharply between standard HUD-code manufactured homes and industrialized modular housing. If a developer wishes to place an industrialized modular home, they must submit a pre-application for a median value assessment, which costs $75. Installing a standard manufactured home in Seguin's Agricultural-Residential (A-R) district requires the applicant to secure a Limited Use Permit ($200) and undergo a full site plan review, which costs $350 for parcels under 1 acre and scales up to $1,000 for parcels over 20 acres.   

6.4 Kyle and San Marcos (Hays County)

Situated on the highly congested IH-35 corridor between San Antonio and Austin, these jurisdictions view oversize transport as a major disruption to public infrastructure and regulate it accordingly.

Kyle: The City of Kyle charges a base moving structure permit fee of $126.79 to bring a home into the city. Due to traffic density, the city frequently mandates a police escort, which is billed in advance at an amount equal to 1.5 times the hourly cost for both the police officer (including wages and benefits) and the patrol car. The subsequent installation permit for the home itself is $57.05, with additional fees assessed for plumbing and electrical connections.   

San Marcos: The Mobile Home Installation Permit in San Marcos is $105, plus a $15 technology fee. San Marcos strictly regulates floodplain development. Any building permit on a lot where the land is not already two feet higher than the city-regulated floodplain requires a Floodplain Permit and rigorous hydraulic modeling. The city will provide this modeling for a premium fee if the homeowner declines to hire a private engineering firm, though the city does not guarantee the model will result in an approval.   

6.5 Schertz (Guadalupe/Bexar/Comal Counties)

Schertz assesses residential building permit fees at a base rate of $0.50 per square foot, making it one of the more expensive jurisdictions based purely on the home's physical footprint. Electrical, plumbing, and mechanical trade permits carry a flat fee of $100 per unit for new construction. Properties located within the MHP (Manufactured Home Park) district are subject to specific dimensional regulations, including a 25-foot front yard setback and a 35-foot maximum building height limit.   

7. Interstate and International Transit Constraints

San Antonio’s strategic location in South Texas makes it a primary logistics clearinghouse for manufactured homes built out-of-state or transiting through the US-Mexico border. Moving an oversize load across state lines dramatically alters the permitting landscape, as each state maintains sovereign authority over its highway infrastructure.

7.1 Louisiana (LaDOTD)

Moves originating in or destined for Louisiana operate through the LaGeaux online permitting system. The Louisiana Department of Transportation and Development (LaDOTD) treats mobile homes uniquely compared to other oversize freight, granting a 3-day validity window for a $10 Oversize Single-Trip Permit (whereas most oversize permits are only valid for a single day). If the transport vehicle exceeds allowable weight limits, pricing scales dynamically based on Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW) and the exact mileage traversed. Furthermore, unregistered out-of-state carriers lacking IRP apportioned registration must purchase a $50 48-hour trip permit from the Office of Motor Vehicles to legally operate within Louisiana.   

7.2 New Mexico (NMDOT)

The New Mexico Department of Transportation (NMDOT) exercises strict oversight over manufactured housing transit. A single-trip oversize/overweight permit requires a $25 base fee plus an aggressive mileage multiplier: $0.025 for every 2,000 pounds over 86,400 pounds, multiplied by the number of miles traveled on New Mexico highways.   

Crucially, NMDOT operates a unique inter-agency data-sharing mandate: whenever a permit is issued for a manufactured home, the issuing authority must immediately forward the permit information (including the origin, destination, and owner's name) directly to the property tax division of the taxation and revenue department, tightly coupling transport logistics with county ad valorem tax collection. Escort rules are also tighter than in Texas: loads exceeding 12 feet in width require at least one escort, and loads over 14 feet require two. If a homeowner intends to self-install the home upon arrival in New Mexico, the state requires them to pass a $50 written examination to prove structural and engineering competency before a permit is issued.   

7.3 Oklahoma (ODOT)

The Oklahoma Department of Transportation (ODOT) assesses a standard $40 fee for single-trip oversize/overweight permits. However, manufactured items that push the boundaries of extreme size—specifically those exceeding 16 feet but not exceeding 23 feet in width—trigger a "Special Movement Permit" requirement. This specialized permit commands a steep $500 fee, reflecting the significant disruption such wide loads cause on the highway system.   

7.4 US-Mexico Border Crossings (Laredo/Hidalgo)

With Laredo serving as North America's premier inland port, the movement of raw materials, modular components, and finished housing units across the border is heavily regulated by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) and TxDMV.

Under the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) frameworks, Mexican commercial vehicles operating exclusively within designated Border Commercial Zones (e.g., 8 miles from the Laredo city limits, 15 miles from El Paso) are exempt from certain temporary registration requirements. However, to push inland to the San Antonio MSA, Mexican-domiciled carriers must possess a US DOT number, an FMCSA-assigned MX number, and apply for either a TxDMV 72-hour permit, a 144-hour permit, or a NAFTA Annual Permit to establish legal registration.   

For trailers crossing the border, Mexico's Temporary Import Permit (TIP) rules dictate logistics. Under Rule 3.4.8 of the Mexican Foreign Trade General Rules for 2025, US trailers can circulate within the Mexican border zone without undergoing a separate, costly temporary importation process, provided they display valid US license plates and a non-Mexican resident is actively on board the vehicle during its operation. Toll friction is also a significant factor; crossing the Laredo International bridges (such as the World Trade Bridge) costs $4.75 per axle for commercial cargo, exclusive of any additional oversize permit surcharges.   

8. Carrier Licensing, Bonding, and Insurance Capitalization

The financial and legal barriers to entry for manufactured home carriers operating in Texas are distinct from standard freight carriers. Because the delivery of a manufactured home inextricably links highway transportation with structural installation and consumer retail, operators are governed simultaneously by the FMCSA, the TxDMV, and the TDHCA.

8.1 Statutory Preemption of Local Authority

A critical piece of the capitalization puzzle is defined by Texas Occupations Code § 1201.008(d). This statute explicitly preempts local governmental units (such as the City of San Antonio, Bexar County, or Boerne) from demanding their own distinct permits, fees, bonds, or insurance policies specifically for the transportation and installation of a manufactured home by a state-licensed retailer or installer. Municipalities may only collect the "actual costs incurred" that result directly from the transportation event (e.g., billing the carrier for the hourly cost of a police escort or the cost of a utility crew raising traffic lights). This legislative preemption is designed to prevent municipalities from stacking arbitrary, localized insurance and bonding mandates on top of state requirements as a backdoor method to discourage affordable housing development.   

8.2 State-Level Bond Minimums (TDHCA & TxDMV)

Transporting a manufactured home usually involves the physical placement and stabilization of the unit at the destination, meaning the carrier must be capitalized and bonded in accordance with TDHCA licensing tiers :   

Installer (I): Requires a $25,000 Continuous Manufactured Housing Licensing Surety Bond, payable to the Manufactured Homeowner Consumer Claims Program.   

Broker (B) / Retailer (R): Requires a $50,000 Surety Bond.   

Manufacturer: Requires a $100,000 Surety Bond. If an out-of-state manufacturer lacks a licensed facility inside Texas from which warranty service can be provided, they must post an additional $100,000 bond (totaling $200,000) to guarantee consumer protection.   

In addition to the consumer protection bonds required by the TDHCA, carriers operating strictly as specialized freight haulers who are exempt from FMCSA Unified Carrier Registration (UCR) must file a $10,000 Permit Surety Bond (Form 439) directly with the TxDMV. This bond is payable to TxDOT and is designed to cover any potential physical damage that the oversize equipment might cause to the state highway infrastructure.   

8.3 Insurance Minimums

Insurance capitalization operates on a dual-track system involving both federal and state mandates:

Federal (FMCSA): Freight forwarders and brokers of property must maintain a $75,000 Surety Bond or Trust Fund Agreement to secure and maintain their federal operating authority.   

State (TxDMV): To execute a move—even in cases where a homeowner is moving their own home using a leased towing vehicle—the state mandates minimum liability insurance of $300,000 combined single limit. For commercial motor carriers operating under standard TxDMV authority, general liability minimums apply (typically $30,000/$60,000/$25,000 for standard vehicles), though the massive size, weight, and valuation of manufactured homes inherently command specialized inland marine and oversize cargo policies that scale well beyond the statutory minimums.   

Conclusion

The 2026 market landscape for manufactured home transport within the San Antonio MSA is defined by a rigid sequence of state oversight, logistical bottlenecking, and highly fragmented municipal land-use laws. Carriers must expertly orchestrate TDHCA licensing, adhere to exact 7-day state reporting windows for structural inspections, and navigate TxPROS oversize load routing, all while dodging severe urban curfews inside Loop 1604 and weekend transit blackouts on IH-35. Furthermore, as operators cross from unincorporated Bexar County into adjacent jurisdictions like Boerne or San Marcos, they face escalating trade permit costs, flood modeling requirements, and exclusionary zoning practices disguised as architectural standards.

Success in this specialized freight market requires extreme administrative agility, robust financial capitalization to meet layered federal and state bond requirements, and precise, real-time coordination with local law enforcement to ensure seamless, compliant, and profitable delivery.
