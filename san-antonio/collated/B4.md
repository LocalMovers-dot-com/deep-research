# Seasonality & Demand Drivers — San Antonio Collated Research (2026)

## Overview
This prompt researched month-by-month pricing seasonality for manufactured home transport in San Antonio, along with the specific local demand drivers: JBSA military PCS cycle, severe weather/FEMA deployments, population growth, Eagle Ford Shale workforce housing, park closures/displacement, and retirement migration.

### Gemini Summary
- Provided the most aggressive seasonal multiplier range (0.82x to 1.30x = 48% spread) with detailed month-by-month narratives
- Estimated 225-315 JBSA-related manufactured home moves annually based on 5-7% of PCS moves involving MH
- Detailed 6 distinct demand drivers: FEMA, military PCS, population boom, Eagle Ford Shale, construction displacement, and retirement/snowbird
- Covered SB 785 zoning reform (effective late 2025/2026) opening more zones to manufactured housing, expected to increase transport demand
- Provided specific TxDOT regulatory constraints: daylight-only movement, 7 AM-9 AM and 4 PM-6 PM curfews, and weekend premium of 10-30%

### Claude Summary
- Most data-rich with specific figures: 23,945 new SA residents in 2023-24, SA now #6 U.S. city at ~1.57 million, metro approaching 2.8 million
- Emphatically corrected the snowbird narrative: SA is NOT a Winter Texan destination (that is the Rio Grande Valley with ~100,000 seasonal visitors); SA is a permanent retirement destination (#2 nationally)
- Provided the most detail on severe weather: 222 hail reports in 12 months, $3.7 billion April 2021 hailstorm, July 2025 Hill Country floods (135+ killed), June 2025 SA floods (3.98 in/hr record)
- Identified the Pentagon PCS budget 50% cut order (May 28, 2025) as a potential future demand reducer
- TMHA shipment data: 18,343 TX units in 2024, forecast 15,882 for 2026; dealer inventory at 14,700 unsold units

### ChatGPT Summary
- Most conservative multiplier range (0.86x to 1.18x = 32% spread) -- explicitly stated as "evidence-based market model" rather than observed data
- Provided the most rigorous methodology disclosure: acknowledged no published SA-specific monthly price index exists; built model from measurable demand/capacity drivers
- Focused on DoD PCS timing references with specific military publication citations (Army 2013, Air Force 2012, JBSA 2018)
- Covered the TDHCA Region 9 housing report showing Eagle Ford impact: manufactured home parks at 98.4% occupancy, rents doubling/tripling
- Most careful treatment of the "how many PCS moves involve manufactured homes" question: noted DTR Part IV Attachment K5 allows MH in lieu of HHG but no JBSA-specific count exists

## Areas of Agreement
- All three platforms identify January as the cheapest month and July as the most expensive
- All cite ~4,500 JBSA peak-season household goods shipments (May-August) as a key demand driver
- All reference the FEMA Harvey MHU overestimate (~2,600 units, ~$152 million) as evidence of disaster-response capacity diversion
- All identify park closures (9+ since 2014) as a displacement-driven demand source
- All agree the Eagle Ford Shale creates workforce housing demand that competes for transport capacity
- All agree that winter months offer the lowest pricing due to compressed demand

## Areas of Divergence
- Seasonal spread magnitude: Gemini (48%), Claude (40%), ChatGPT (32%) -- Gemini is most aggressive, ChatGPT most conservative
- JBSA MH move estimates: Gemini 225-315/yr (5-7% of PCS), Claude 100-480/yr (1-3% of PCS), ChatGPT declined to estimate a specific number
- Snowbird impact: Gemini says moderate (retiree inbound), Claude says emphatically no (snowbirds go to RGV), ChatGPT says secondary -- Claude's correction is well-sourced
- I-35 NEX project cost: Gemini says $1.9B total; Claude says $1.5B for just the central phase -- not necessarily contradictory (different scope)
- Only Claude covered the Pentagon PCS budget cut order and its potential long-term impact

## Recommended Data for LocalMovers.com
- **Seasonal pricing guidance**: Use Claude's 0.80x-1.20x range as the primary consumer-facing model (40% spread, well-sourced, and the middle ground)
- **Cheapest months**: January-February (save 15-20%); most expensive: June-July (pay 18-20% premium)
- **Optimal scheduling sweet spot**: October-early November or late February-March (moderate weather, past hurricane peak, before spring demand ramp)
- **Best days**: Tuesday-Wednesday mid-week (Gemini's detail on 10-30% weekend premium is valuable consumer guidance)
- **Book 30-45 days ahead** even in off-season -- all platforms indicate SA transporters are typically booked a month out
- **FEMA warning**: Note that Gulf hurricane events (June-November) can suddenly divert transport capacity to disaster response, creating civilian shortages and 20-30% cost spikes
- **Correct the snowbird myth**: SA is a permanent retirement destination, not a seasonal snowbird market -- this matters for year-round demand modeling
- **Population growth driver**: 24,000 new residents/year feeding manufactured housing demand; TX leads nation at 18,343 shipments in 2024
