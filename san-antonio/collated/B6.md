# Hidden Costs & Surcharges — San Antonio Collated Research (2026)

## Overview
This prompt researched the hidden, overlooked, and commonly underquoted costs of moving a manufactured home in San Antonio, including wind zone compliance, Hill Country terrain surcharges, I-35 construction rerouting, CPS Energy/SAWS utility fees, Edwards Aquifer compliance, split geology foundation costs, floodplain elevation mandates, pre-1976 home restrictions, and asbestos abatement.

### Gemini Summary
- Strongest coverage of pre-1976 home issues: 80-90% carrier refusal rate, $3,200-$8,000 structural retrofit costs, and TCEQ asbestos abatement requirements ($250-$800 testing, $2,238 avg abatement)
- Most detailed wind zone analysis: explained WZ I vs WZ II anchoring differences (diagonal tie spacing, vertical ties, centerline anchoring) with specific surcharge of $1,800-$3,700 for WZ II compliance
- Covered the Balcones Fault geological divide: Houston Black clay (east/south) vs. Edwards Plateau limestone (north/west), and how this split requires fundamentally different foundation approaches
- Detailed TCEQ jurisdiction over asbestos-containing materials in pre-1980s homes and the environmental remediation cost structure
- Strongest treatment of the marriage-line splitting process and its hidden complexity for multi-section homes

### Claude Summary
- Most comprehensive single source of hidden cost data with dollar figures for virtually every category, organized in clear cost tables
- Only platform to quantify the total Hill Country delivery surcharge ($1,850-$32,450+ beyond flat-terrain equivalent)
- Provided the most detail on CPS Energy and SAWS fee schedules, including gas reconnection at the main ($400-$600), temporary service ($150-$225), rescheduled connections ($58), and SAWS impact fees ($7,343-$11,528)
- Only platform to cover park-related hidden costs: lot rent ($450-$650), application fees ($25-$50), security deposits, lot prep ($3,000-$7,000), and move-out restoration ($500-$1,500)
- Covered split geology with specific foundation costs: engineered slab $4,000-$10,000 (clay), helical piers $7,500-$40,000 (limestone), foundation repair $5,000-$30,000
- Only platform to detail floodplain elevation costs: fill method $5,000-$15,000+, pier/pile $10,000-$40,000+, MH lifting $10-$18/sqft

### ChatGPT Summary
- Most methodical source-citation approach with per-item dollar ranges tied to specific published sources and access dates
- Only platform to cover Edwards Aquifer Protection Program (WPAP) compliance: $650 fee for SFR under 5 acres, up to $10,000 maximum
- Provided the clearest detour cost model: published per-mile rates for tow + escort, with worked examples for 18-mile and 28-mile reroutes
- Covered CPS Energy fees with the same specificity as Claude ($24-$30 electric, $36-$47 gas, $400-$600 gas main, $58 rescheduled connection)
- Addressed TDHCA cross-drive rock anchor requirements for caliche/limestone soils with retail anchor pricing ($29.95-$39.80 per unit)
- Most careful about not publishing unsourced figures -- declined to estimate CPS line-lift costs because no published per-crossing schedule was found

## Areas of Agreement
- All three platforms confirm Bexar County is Wind Zone I (70 mph standard)
- All agree CPS Energy electric reconnection at meter costs $24-$30 (regular/premium hours)
- All agree CPS gas reconnection at meter costs $36-$47
- All identify Bexar County's split geology (expansive clay east/south, limestone north/west) as a major hidden cost driver
- All agree that the "transport quote" typically excludes foundation work, utility hookups, skirting, and regulatory fees
- All agree the floodplain development permit in Bexar County is $50 per activity

## Areas of Divergence
- Wind zone upgrade cost: Gemini says $1,800-$3,700 for WZ II compliance; Claude says $800-$1,500 premium; Claude also notes a new vertical-anchor standard adding $500-$1,500 even in WZ I (TMHA Nov 2025)
- Edwards Aquifer: Only ChatGPT covered the WPAP filing fee ($650-$10,000) -- this is a significant omission by the other platforms for properties in the recharge zone
- Pre-1976 homes: Only Gemini covered the 80-90% carrier refusal rate and asbestos abatement costs
- Park-related costs: Only Claude covered lot rent, application fees, security deposits, and move-out restoration fees
- Hill Country quantification: Only Claude provided a total surcharge range ($1,850-$32,450+); Gemini discussed terrain challenges qualitatively but did not sum them
