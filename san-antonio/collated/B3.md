# Distance-Based Pricing Tiers — San Antonio Collated Research (2026)

## Overview
This prompt researched how manufactured home transport costs scale with distance in the San Antonio market, broken into six mileage tiers (0-25, 25-50, 50-100, 100-200, 200-500, 500+ miles), with per-mile rates, escort economics, fuel surcharges, and route-specific constraints.

### Gemini Summary
- Provided the most detailed 6-tier pricing matrix with separate tables for base transport, per-mile rates, permit escalation, escort costs, and total all-in cost for each home type
- Introduced the Texas Triangle backhaul optimization concept -- carriers returning from SA to Houston/DFW factories can offer volume discounts, compressing Tier 3-4 pricing
- Most detailed fuel surcharge analysis: March 2026 diesel at $4.859/gal triggered 17% LTL surcharge and $0.33–$0.81/mi TL surcharge
- Covered interstate permit stacking for LA ($10 base + bridge evaluation fees up to $850), NM ($27.95 + $0.25/ton/mi), and OK ($40 + $10/1,000 lbs over legal)
- Emphasized that short-haul per-mile rates appear inflated ($100–$720/mi) because fixed mobilization costs dominate

### Claude Summary
- Cleanest consumer-facing pricing tables with example routes (SA to New Braunfels, SA to Austin, SA to Houston/Corpus)
- Highlighted that TX permit structure is a major cost advantage: flat $40/section regardless of distance (no per-mile surcharge, no per-county fee)
- Most detailed escort threshold analysis specific to manufactured housing (up to 16': no escort, 16'-18': 1 escort, over 18': 2 escorts) with the 1,000-foot convoy sharing provision
- Addressed I-35 NEX construction program ($1.5B+, ~20 miles, through fall 2027) as a major routing constraint adding 10-30 additional miles per north-south move
- Provided SA-specific district restriction details: Loop 410 blanket prohibition, I-35 12' max width, no-permits zones at certain interchanges

### ChatGPT Summary
- Built the most methodical cost model with explicit linehaul, escort, fuel surcharge, and permit columns computed at representative midpoint mileages (15, 40, 75, 150, 350, 750 mi)
- Widest cost ranges reflecting maximum uncertainty: SW 0-25 mi ranged from $1,909 to $8,281 (low end represents transport-ready homes, high end represents complex moves)
- Anchored to published per-mile benchmarks from multiple sources (Extra Space Storage, Jim.com, Pilot Car 101) with explicit publication dates
- Provided a fuel surcharge model using DOE's 2026 weekly matrix: $0.57/mi at $5.071/gal benchmark
- Treated escort costs as $0 to (max range) because many standard sections under 16' do not require escorts in Texas

## Areas of Agreement
- All three platforms agree on the toter truck rate: $4.00–$5.50 per mile per section
- All agree that short-distance per-mile rates appear dramatically inflated due to fixed mobilization costs
- All agree the TxDMV permit is a flat $40/section with no distance escalation -- a Texas cost advantage
- All agree that the 6 national holiday blackouts and daylight-only movement restrictions affect scheduling and cost
- All identify Loop 410 as a major routing barrier for oversize loads in San Antonio

## Areas of Divergence
- Cost range width: ChatGPT's ranges are the widest (reflecting full spectrum from transport-only to complex); Gemini's are the tightest (more prescriptive); Claude falls in between
- Fuel analysis: Gemini cited $4.859 national average; ChatGPT cited $5.071 national and $4.835 Gulf Coast -- possible different week references in March 2026
- Escort pricing: Gemini at $1.85–$2.15/mi; Claude at $1.50–$3.50/mi; ChatGPT at $1.50–$3.50/mi -- Gemini's tighter range may reflect 2026-specific quotes vs. broader published ranges
- Only Gemini covered the Texas Triangle backhaul effect and interstate permit stacking economics in detail
- I-35 width restriction: Gemini says 11' max; Claude says 12' max on the same corridor segment -- may reflect different restriction entries or time periods
